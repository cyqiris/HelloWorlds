# Best estimate of oral SAMe absorption in dogs for Denamarin coated, Denamarin Chewable and Denamarin Advanced Chewable (9.1 kg dog)

Evidence tags used below:
- **[PR]** peer-reviewed paper.
- **[CA]** conference abstract. JVIM states the ACVIM abstracts "are not peer reviewed before publication".
- **[MFR]** manufacturer-authored or manufacturer-funded data.
- **[PAT]** patent.
- **[REG]** regulatory label (SmPC or national instruction).
- **[HUM] / [HORSE] / [RAT]** species other than dog.
- **[EXTRAP]** my own calculation or extrapolation.
- **[SNIP]** search-engine summary only, not verified.

Research done 2026-11-09/10. Unless stated otherwise, "F" means absolute oral bioavailability: the % of the oral dose that reaches the systemic circulation as intact SAMe, measured as oral AUC against IV AUC.

## Q1. Is there any dog study that measured absolute oral bioavailability (oral vs IV) of SAMe?

### Takeaway
**No.** I searched PubMed, Europe PMC, the full text of every ACVIM Forum abstract programme available as open-access text (2014–2026), Google Patents (MSI and BioResearch families), and Chinese, Japanese, Russian and Italian web sources. I found no dog study that gave SAMe both IV and orally, so **no measured absolute oral bioavailability exists for dogs.**

What is new since the first round: two **independent dog oral PK datasets with actual numbers** now exist.
- Nutramax ACVIM 2016 abstract, table images.
- MSI patent US 12,303,524 B2, Table 4.

For tosylate-disulfate SAMe given fasted, both give about **320–325 ng·h/mL of plasma SAMe AUC per mg/kg** of SAMe ion. That allows a dog-anchored "bridged" estimate of F (Q8).

### Cited Findings
**Nutramax 2016 abstract P02 [CA][MFR]: dog AUC for the original tosylate chewable, fasted**
- Design, verbatim: "Six beagles were dosed after an overnight fast with 225 mg SAMe ion (mean 21.7 mg/kg) in one formulation of the supplement, followed by the other formulation 7 days later administered with food."
- Method: "Plasma values for SAMe before dosing and at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 24 hours were determined using an HPLC‐ESI‐MS/MS assay."
- Baseline: "With both supplements SAMe plasma levels increased substantially above baseline values (21–38 ng/mL)".
- Table 2, column "SAMe Tosylate Chewable Tablet (Mean ± Std), Fasted (n=6)":
  - Cmax 1,202 ± 365 ng/ml
  - Tmax 1.5 ± 0.5 hr
  - AUC0-24 7,060 ± 1,431 ng•hr/ml
  - t1/2 9.03 ± 3.39 hr
- Sources:
  - [2016 ACVIM Forum Research Abstract Program, PMC5260423](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5260423/).
  - The table exists only as an image: [Table 2 image](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/f50b/7166465/ec338f0ab3b6/JVIM-30-1407-g014.jpg). I read the image directly.

**MSI patent US 12,303,524 B2 [PAT]: dog AUC for an enteric-coated tosylate-disulfate tablet, fasted**
- Methods, verbatim: "fasted male beagle dogs (7-10 kg) were used… Each group (consisting of 6 dogs) was dosed with a single orally administered enteric coated tablet, under fasted conditions".
- Tablets: "400 mg SAMe ion (from SAMe disulfate tosylate)".
- Sampling and assay: "pre-dose, 20 and 40 minutes, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8 hours", by "LC-ESI-MS/MS".
- Table 4, control row (400 mg SAMe, 0 mg propyl gallate):
  - Tmax 1.8 ± 0.7 h
  - Cmax 6,731 ng/mL
  - AUC(0-t) 14,756 hr·ng/mL
  - AUC(0-inf) 15,186 ± 6,831 hr·ng/mL
  - T1/2 1.4 ± 0.4 h
- With propyl gallate (6.25, 25 or 100 mg), AUC(0-inf) rose to 21,734, 25,159 and 34,305 hr·ng/mL.
- The patent also states: "AUC values are preferably calculated from 0-24 hours using the trapezoid method and are uncorrected for baseline, endogenous SAMe levels."
- Source: [Google Patents US12303524B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12303524B2/en) (MSI-195 Holdings LLC; priority 2012-10-17; granted 2025-05-20).
- The patent's figures also include "a commercially available oral formulation of SAMe" in beagles at 400 mg, but values for it are given only graphically ([same patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12303524B2/en)).

### Inferences
- **Dose-normalised dog exposure [EXTRAP].**
  - Nutramax tosylate chewable: 7,060 / 21.7 = **325 ng·h/mL per mg/kg** (this AUC0-24 may include endogenous baseline).
  - MSI enteric-coated tosylate tablet: 15,186 / (400 mg ÷ dog weight). That is **266–380 per mg/kg** for 7–10 kg dogs, and 323 at an assumed 8.5 kg.
  - Two independent dog datasets, one uncoated chewable and one enteric-coated tablet, therefore give essentially the same exposure per mg/kg. This independently supports Nutramax's unpublished claim that chewable and coated bioavailability are "comparable".
- **Half-life discrepancy [EXTRAP].** Nutramax's t1/2 of about 9 h is much longer than MSI's 1.4 h. Nutramax's 24-h AUC probably contains endogenous baseline: 21–38 ng/mL × 24 h ≈ 500–900 ng·h/mL, or 7–13% of 7,060. A baseline-corrected value of about 290–300 per mg/kg is therefore used as the lower dog value below.

### Gaps
- No dog IV SAMe data at all: no dog clearance, volume or IV half-life. This is the single missing number that prevents a measured dog F.
- Not directly searchable from here: CNKI, Wanfang and J-STAGE (general web search only). Stramentinoli & Catto 1976 full text (Elsevier) was not accessible.
- The 2009 ACVIM abstract cited by Skorupski 2011 (Griffin, Whalen, Filburn, "Bioavailability of a novel formulation of s-adenosylmethionine in Beagle dogs") could not be found. The 2009 ACVIM abstract programme is not indexed in Europe PMC. The 2002, 2007, 2008 and 2010–2013 programmes are listed there but have no open-access text, so they could not be scanned.

## Q2. ACVIM Forum abstracts: exact numbers, and per-mg relative bioavailability of phytate (NMXSS75A) vs tosylate (NMXSS75)

### Takeaway
- **2016 P02 has real numbers, in table images.** NMXSS75A chewable given **with food**, at apparently the same 225 mg, gave AUC0-24 4,579 ± 1,454 vs 7,060 ± 1,431 ng·h/mL for the tosylate chewable given **fasted**. Per mg, phytate-fed was **0.65× tosylate-fasted** by AUC and 0.96× by Cmax, reported as "no statistically significant differences" (n=6).
  - A second table shows food cut tosylate Cmax by **59% (100 g food) and 68% (225 g food)**.
- **2019 NM07 (n=18 crossover) gives no numbers.** It says 82 mg phytate was "comparable" to 225 mg tosylate. If AUCs were equal, that implies a per-mg relative bioavailability of **225/82 = 2.74×**. This is an inference from the dose ratio; the abstract reports no AUC, CI or fasting state.
- **New, independent: 2026 abstract E10 (University of Pennsylvania, not Nutramax).** It measured the first absolute F for NMXSS75A in any species: **1.2% (range 1–2%) in fed horses**, oral 20 mg/kg vs IV SAMe tosylate.
- **2025 abstract E43 (Nutramax, horses)** gives NMXSS75A dose and food data.
- **No other SAMe PK abstracts** were found in the 2014–2026 ACVIM programmes.

### Cited Findings

- Title: "Bioavailability of a Novel Formulation of S‐Adenosylmethioine Given With Food in Beagle Dogs" (sic).
- Design: "In a blinded crossover study a new Denamarin® … chewable tablet containing a novel formulation of SAMe salt (NMXSS75A™) was administered to beagle dogs to assess its bioavailability relative to administration with food."
- Statistics: "Mean values for Cmax ,Tmax, AUC0–24 and t1/2 were comparable for both formulations with no statistically significant differences (Table 2)." The test used is not stated.

- Title: "A Novel New S‐Adenosylmethionine Salt with Increased Bioavailability".
- Objective: "to assess and compare the pharmacokinetic profiles of a 225 mg dose of SAMe tosylate disulfate NMXSS75® (Denamarin®…) and a 82 mg dose of SAMe phytate NMXSS75A™ (Denamarin Advanced…)".

### Inferences
**Per-mg relative bioavailability, phytate vs tosylate [EXTRAP]**

| Source | Basis | Per-mg ratio, phytate : tosylate |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 P02, dog | Phytate fed vs tosylate fasted, same 225 mg, AUC: 4,579 / 7,060 | **0.65** (Cmax 1,157 / 1,202 = 0.96) |
| 2019 NM07, dog | "Comparable" PK at 82 vs 225 mg; equal AUC assumed: 225 / 82 | **2.74** (no numbers to check; "no significant difference" is not equivalence, and the CI is unknown) |
| 2017 brochure, dog | Cmax only | 2.40 (8 vs 20 mg/kg chart) to 4.35 (same-dose chart) |

- **The two Nutramax abstracts are reconcilable only if food reduces phytate absorption a lot.** If phytate is about 2.7× tosylate per mg when both are fasted, the 2016 result implies food cut phytate AUC by about 76% (0.65 / 2.74 = 0.24). That is similar to the 59–68% Cmax cut food caused for tosylate.
  - So the 2016 "equivalent bioavailability when administered with food" finding is better read as "phytate's per-mg advantage roughly offsets a large food penalty", not "phytate is food-insensitive".
  - The horse data (−15% AUC with a small meal) suggest a smaller food effect, but they come from another species and another dosing route (nasogastric).
- **The UPenn horse result is the only absolute F for NMXSS75A.** At 1.2% in fed horses, it shows that even the "enhanced" salt yields single-digit-% systemic bioavailability in at least one species under fed conditions.

### Gaps
- No AUC, Cmax or CI numbers for the 2019 dog crossover (the key dog phytate-vs-tosylate comparison), and its fasting state and tablet forms are not stated.
- Not stated in 2016 Table 1: dose, n, sampling, or AUC.
- Not stated in 2016 Table 2: the amount of food in the "Fed" arm. Whether the 2016 sequence was randomised is also not stated.
- No fasted-dog phytate data at a known dose with numbers.

## Q3. Center et al. 2005 (AJVR 66:330) and Stramentinoli 1987 (Am J Med 83(5A):35-42): any dog or human oral bioavailability numbers?

### Takeaway
- **Center 2005:** neither the full text nor any numeric plasma SAMe values could be obtained.
  - avmajournals.avma.org returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool and a bot-check page to curl.
  - Semantic Scholar lists it as a free-to-read ("bronze") PDF on the AVMA site, so a person with an ordinary browser can probably download it.
- **Stramentinoli 1987:** closed access; no abstract-level numbers.
- Neither source contributes a dog or human F% here.

### Cited Findings
- **Center 2005, abstract only [PR]:**
  - Design: 12 dogs, SAMe "20 mg/kg/d divided in 2 doses".
  - Result: "The stable salt of SAMe was biologically available; plasma concentrations of SAMe or prednisolone were not affected by coadministration."
  - Sources: [PubMed 15757136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15757136/); open-access location per [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.2460/ajvr.2005.66.330?fields=title,openAccessPdf), which lists `https://avmajournals.avma.org/downloadpdf/journals/ajvr/66/2/ajvr.2005.66.330.pdf` as "BRONZE".
- **Denamarin coated insert's use of Center 2005 [MFR, citing PR]:** "In a pharmacokinetic study with fasted dogs given S-Adenosylmethionine, peak plasma levels were recorded in most dogs within 4 hours of administration." — [Denamarin package insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denamarin.pdf).
- **Stramentinoli 1987 [PR]:** closed access; Semantic Scholar marks it "CLOSED". The abstract says only that "data are reported on plasma kinetics, distribution, and metabolism of SAMe after oral administration" — [PubMed 3318439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3318439/); [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/0002-9343(87)90849-7?fields=title,openAccessPdf).

### Inferences
- Center 2005 sampled at most a few time points around twice-daily dosing, as the label's "within 4 hours" wording suggests. Even with the full text it is unlikely to yield an AUC-based F, because there was no IV arm.

### Gaps
- No Cmax, AUC or variability values from Center 2005.
- Stramentinoli 1987's species data are unread.

## Q4. Human ademetionine labels: stated oral bioavailability % and food statements (verbatim)

### Takeaway
The current Italian SmPC for Transmetil and the current Russian Heptral tablet instruction (updated 2026) **give no oral bioavailability %**. Both say oral bioavailability is **higher when fasting**, and the Italian SmPC says tablets "non devono essere assunte ai pasti" (should not be taken with meals).

An **older Abbott Heptral label** (Russia, updated 2020, injection page) states **oral bioavailability 5%** (IM 95%). No study is cited for the 5%. It is higher than the only modern oral-vs-IV measurement (2.1–2.6%; Yang 2009).

I could not open the Chinese 思美泰 insert (sites blocked by human-verification pages).

## Q7. Absorbed in any form vs intact SAMe reaching blood; how much SAMe does the liver receive?

### Takeaway
- **Most of the dose is absorbed in some form.** In human tracer work, only 23.5% of an oral [methyl-14C] label was recovered in faeces by 72 h, and about 60% was retained in body pools.
- **Very little arrives intact.** Intact SAMe reaching the systemic circulation is only about 2–3% (Yang 2009).
- **What explains the gap?** The older literature blamed first-pass hepatic uptake. Later work points to poor intestinal permeability (paracellular transport only) and degradation before or during absorption, and IV clearance arithmetic agrees: SAMe's systemic clearance is far below hepatic blood flow, so the liver probably cannot remove more than about 5–20% on first pass.
- **So the liver probably receives intact SAMe on the same order as the systemic F** (roughly 1.05–1.25× F). It does not receive tens of percent of the dose. The rest reaches tissues, if at all, as breakdown products (MTA, methionine, adenine and methyl groups).
- No direct portal-vein measurement exists for dogs.

### Cited Findings
- **Human tracer data [REG][HUM]:** "в моче было обнаружено 15.5±1.5% радиоактивности через 48 ч, a в кале - 23.5±3.5%…"; "около 60% было задепонировано." — [Vidal Heptral tablets 2026](https://www.vidal.ru/drugs/heptral__2338).
- **Human IV kinetics [PR][HUM]:** "apparent volumes of distribution… 407 +/- 27 and 443 +/- 36 ml/kg… terminal half-lives 81 +/- 8 and 101 +/- 7 min and body clearances 3.7 +/- 0.5 and 3.1 +/- 0.2 ml/min per kg. Urinary excretion was 34 +/- 3 and 40 +/- 2% of the administered dose… drug disposition occurs more via metabolism than via renal excretion" — [Giulidori et al. 1984, PubMed 6489422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6489422/).
- **Older first-pass interpretation [PR][RAT]:** "low plasma levels after the oral administration may be suggestive of a first-pass effect of SAMe characterized by an extensive uptake of the drug by the liver where it is rapidly metabolized." — [Stramentinoli et al. 1979, PubMed 439007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/439007/).
- **Same view in liver patients [PR][HUM]:** "Exogenous SAMe is stable in digestive juices and, although well absorbed orally, bioavailability is reduced because of a significant first pass effect in the liver." — [Kaye et al. 1990, PubMed 2081477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2081477/).
- **Isolated perfused rat liver [PR][RAT]:** "the uptake of the labelled methyl group by hepatic tissue of fed rats was low (6%). This value increased to 12% in organs isolated from 24-hour fasted animals." — [Guaitani et al. 1979, PubMed 535598](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/535598/).
- **Permeability-limited view, Caco-2 [PR, in vitro]:** "apparent permeability coefficients… (0.7x10(-6) and 0.6x10(-6) cm s-1…), i.e. identical to that of the paracellular transport marker mannitol"; hepatocyte "Accumulation… was very low, with a cell:medium ratio at equilibrium of only 0.2-0.5." — [McMillan et al. 2005, PubMed 15901349](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15901349/).
- **Same view [PR][RAT]:** "the poor oral bioavailability shown by SAM can be correlated to absorption problems of the molecule, rather than to its rapid metabolism (McMillan et al. 2005)" — [Francioso et al. 2021, PMC8519898](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8519898/).
- **Portal entry shown in humans [PR][HUM]:** after direct jejunal injection, "SAM concentrations increased significantly in venous mesenteric and systemic blood" — [Bombardieri et al. 1983, PubMed 6862722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6862722/).
- **Uncertainty about tracer meaning [PR review]:** "it is unclear whether such incorporation occurred through intact SAM or through its degradation products." — [Sun & Locasale 2021, PMC8728568](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728568/).

### Inferences
**Hepatic extraction estimate [EXTRAP, human-based, model-dependent]**
- Inputs:
  - human plasma clearance 1.5–3.7 mL/min/kg (Giulidori; Yang IV, my calculation in Q8);
  - about 34–40% of that clearance is renal;
  - so non-renal clearance is about 0.9–2.4 mL/min/kg.
- Assumption: human hepatic blood flow is about 20 mL/min/kg (a standard physiology value, not from the sources above).
- Result: even if all non-renal clearance were hepatic, hepatic extraction would be about **0.05–0.12**, or **≤ about 0.2** if SAMe stays out of red cells (blood/plasma ratio about 0.6).
- Consequence: intact SAMe in portal blood ≈ F / (1 − E_H) ≈ **1.05–1.25 × F**. With F ≈ 3% in dogs (Q8), the liver would receive roughly **3–4% of the dose as intact SAMe on first pass**, about 7–8 mg from a 225 mg tablet.

**Why this matters**
- The "~60% retained" tracer figure describes the methyl label in any chemical form, not SAMe.
- It should not be used as an "absorption rate" for SAMe.

### Gaps
- No dog data on portal-vein SAMe, tracer balance or hepatic extraction.
- The BioResearch rat portal/aortic data in EP0136464A2 were not extractable as numbers.
- Hepatic blood flow and SAMe blood/plasma partitioning are assumptions, not sourced values.

- **Unpublished dog data that would narrow the C estimate:** numbers from the 2019 NM07 crossover (AUC ratio and 90% CI), and from the 2008 chewable-vs-coated study (data on file).
- **Advanced Chewable vs hard tablet:** no data on whether the 120 mg chewable performs like the 82 mg hard tablet tested in 2019 (the strength of the medium-dog hard tablet).
