## Q1. Per-tablet active ingredients, amounts and declared SAMe form for every size of every line

### Takeaway
Nutramax currently lists **four formulations**: (A) original enteric-coated tablets, (B) original chewable tablets (one size), (C1) Advanced Chewable tablets (2 sizes) and (C2) Advanced Hard tablets (3 sizes). Every size of every line declares exactly two actives: **S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe)** and **silybin-phosphatidylcholine complex (SPC), with the silybin A+B content stated separately**. No line lists vitamin E, zinc or any other botanical. The SAMe salt is named only as Nutramax trade specifications: **NMXSS75®** in the original chewable and **NMXSS75A®** in Advanced. No label I could access names a chemical salt such as "tosylate disulfate", and none says whether the mg figure is expressed as SAMe ion.

### Inferences
- **SPC is about 29–30% silybin by weight in every product.** Calculated silybin ÷ SPC: 9/31 = 29.0%, 24/82 = 29.3%, 35/120 = 29.2%, 56/190 = 29.5%, 35/118 = 29.7%, 70/235 = 29.8%, 24/84 = 28.6%, 35/123 = 28.5%. So "82 mg SPC" means 24 mg silybin, not 82 mg.
- **The patents are not about the new salt.** The patents printed on the Advanced flyer cover the SAMe + milk thistle/silybin combination concept from 1998. They are not patents on the NMXSS75A salt. Nothing I found shows that NMXSS75A itself is patented; it is described only as "proprietary".
- **The vet's email matches the label exactly.** "SAMe 225 mg + SPC 82 mg providing 24 mg silybin A+B" is the official medium coated tablet, sold as bottle 3000111 or blister pack DENAMMD30.

### Gaps
- **SAMe chemical form.** No accessible Nutramax or retailer label text names the SAMe salt chemically (e.g., tosylate disulfate or butanedisulfonate). None states whether the mg values are SAMe-ion equivalents. The coated tablets' SAMe is not labelled NMXSS75 on any page I saw. Full label/insert text on drugs.com/vet was blocked (403), and physical package back panels were not available.
- **"Extra large dogs" coated tablet (675 mg SAMe / 190 mg SPC / 56 mg silybin).** It appears only in the denamarin.com dosing table. There is no SKU in the page's product data, I found no US retailer listing, and Plumb's 2024 handout lists only 90/225/425 mg coated tablets. Whether it is sold in the US (or is new or planned) is unverified.
- US 6,863,906 could not be retrieved from Google Patents (rate-limited).

## Q2. Inactive ingredients, flavors and excipients

### Takeaway
Nutramax does **not publish inactive-ingredient lists** for any Denamarin line. What is disclosed second-hand:
- **Original chewable:** contains **hydrolyzed chicken flavor** and **a small amount of a sugar-derivative sweetening enhancer**.
- **Advanced Chewable:** "supplied in a hydrolyzed chicken flavor".
- **Coated tablets:** contain **no chicken**.
- **Advanced Hard tablets:** "Does not contain any dyes".

## Q3. Label dosing by body weight, and what a 9.1 kg (20 lb) dog gets in each line

### Takeaway
At 20 lb (9.07 kg) the label doses are:
- **(A) coated:** 1 Medium tablet → 225 mg SAMe + 82 mg SPC (24 mg silybin).
- **(B) chewable:** 1 tablet → 225 mg SAMe + 82 mg SPC (24 mg silybin), the same mg as A.
- **(C1) Advanced Chewable:** ½ Small-to-Medium tablet → 60 mg SAMe (NMXSS75A) + 59 mg SPC (17.5 mg silybin).
- **(C2) Advanced Hard:** 1 Medium tablet → 82 mg SAMe (NMXSS75A) + 84 mg SPC (24 mg silybin).

No line distinguishes an initial dose from a maintenance dose. Every page says the number of tablets "may be gradually reduced, or increased, at any time at the recommendation of your veterinarian".

### Cited Findings
**Dosing tables quoted from the official pages:**
- **A, coated** ([OFFICIAL](https://www.denamarin.com/coated-tablets)):

  - Medium dogs: "13 - 34 lbs (6 - 15.5 kg) 1"
  - Large dogs: "35 - 65 lbs (16 - 29.5 kg) 1"; "66 - 120 lbs (30 - 54.5 kg) 2"; "Over 120 lbs (54.5 kg) 3"

### Inferences
**Daily intake at label dose for a 9.1 kg / 20 lb dog** (arithmetic from the official per-tablet values):

| Line, size, label dose | SAMe/day | SAMe mg/kg | SPC/day | Silybin A+B/day | Silybin mg/kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Coated Medium, 1 tablet (vet's prescription) | 225 mg | 24.7 | 82 mg | 24 mg | 2.6 |
| B. Original Chewable, 1 tablet (16–30 lb band) | 225 mg (NMXSS75) | 24.7 | 82 mg | 24 mg | 2.6 |
| C1. Advanced Chewable S/M, ½ tablet (15–25 lb band) | 60 mg (NMXSS75A) | 6.6 | 59 mg | 17.5 mg | 1.9 |
| C2. Advanced Hard Medium, 1 tablet (12–34 lb band) | 82 mg (NMXSS75A) | 9.0 | 84 mg | 24 mg | 2.6 |
| C2 at ½ tablet (below the label dose for 20 lb) | 41 mg | 4.5 | 42 mg | 12 mg | 1.3 |

- **The original chewable reproduces the prescription on paper.** At 20 lb, B is labelled at exactly the same daily mg of SAMe, SPC and silybin as the prescribed coated Medium tablet (A).
- **The dog's previous "half a tablet daily" was most likely the Advanced Chewable Small-to-Medium.** That is the only Advanced product whose label dose for a 15–25 lb dog is ½ tablet. The Advanced Hard Medium label dose for 12–34 lb is 1 whole tablet, and the hard tablet is not described as scored. Treat this as inference until the bottle or SKU is confirmed (DENAMCHWSM30-ADV versus DENAMMD30-ADV).
- **The flyer's equivalence ratio, applied to this dog.** By the manufacturer's own ratio (NMXSS75A 8 mg/kg ≈ NMXSS75 20 mg/kg for Cmax, i.e. ×2.5):
  - 60 mg NMXSS75A/day would be roughly Cmax-comparable to about 150 mg NMXSS75.
  - 82 mg would be roughly comparable to about 205 mg.

  This is an extrapolation from unpublished manufacturer data. It covers only Cmax (peak level), not exposure over time (AUC). The flyer compares against the chewable's NMXSS75 salt; the coated tablet's salt is not named.

### Gaps
- No official initial-versus-maintenance (loading) schedule exists for any line.
- There is no label band for dogs under 7 lb on Advanced Chewable, and no Advanced Chewable dose between ½ and 1 tablet for 26–50 lb.

## Q4. Administration: empty stomach vs food; whether tablets may be split, crushed or chewed; scoring; packaging; storage

### Takeaway
- **All four lines:** the label says give **on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal**.
- **Advanced lines only (C1, C2):** add "If pilling ... is not possible, the tablet may be administered with a small amount of food."
- **Original lines (A, B):** have no food allowance.
- **Coated tablets (A):** must be swallowed whole. Nutramax says they "should generally not be broken", with dose changes "in full tablet increments". Retailer and Plumb's text: "should not be split or crumbled" / "Do not crush or split enteric-coated tablets".
- **Both chewable lines (B, C1):** are **scored** and labelled in ¼ or ½ fractions, so splitting is intended.
- **Advanced Hard (C2):** is dosed only in whole tablets and is not described as scored.

## Q8. Terminology: is milk thistle the same as silybin? Conversions, and what Denamarin's "silybin A+B in phosphatidylcholine complex" is

### Takeaway
**No.** Milk thistle (*Silybum marianum*) is the plant. **Silymarin** is the flavonolignan mixture extracted from its fruits or seeds: silybin A/B, isosilybin A/B, silychristin, isosilychristin, silydianin, plus the flavonoid taxifolin. **Silybin (= silibinin = silybin A + silybin B, a 1:1 diastereomer pair)** is the main constituent, about **50–70% of silymarin** (USP extract spec: 40–65% silybin A+B within the silymarin). Most commercial extracts are standardized to **about 80% (sometimes 70%) silymarin**. So "X mg milk thistle extract (80% silymarin)" ≈ 0.8X mg silymarin ≈ **0.32X–0.56X mg silybin**, not X mg. Unextracted seed contains ≤ about 6% silymarin.

Denamarin's ingredient is a **silybin–phosphatidylcholine complex (phytosome)**, the same concept as Indena's Siliphos / silipide / IdB 1016. By the label numbers it is about 29–30% silybin by weight, so 82 mg SPC = 24 mg silybin. Nutramax's **Marin** uses silybin in a phosphatidylcholine complex plus vitamin E and zinc, with no SAMe.

### Inferences
**Conversion rules for comparing labels:**
- *"Milk thistle extract X mg" (80% silymarin)* → about 0.8X mg silymarin → silybin A+B about 0.32X–0.52X mg (USP 40–65% of silymarin) or 0.40X–0.56X mg (Hackett 50–70%). Example: 100 mg extract ≈ 32–56 mg silybin. At 70% silymarin: about 0.28X–0.49X mg.
- *"Silymarin X mg"* → silybin about 0.4X–0.7X mg.
- *"Milk thistle seed/herb powder X mg"* (unextracted) → silymarin ≤ about 0.06X mg → silybin ≤ about 0.04X mg.
- *"SPC / silybin phytosome X mg"* (Denamarin-type) → silybin about 0.29–0.30X mg. So 82 mg SPC = 24 mg silybin; 118 mg = 35 mg.

**Bioavailability:** even equal silybin mg are not equally absorbed. Filburn's dog data show SPC gave about 2.8× the Cmax and about 3.0–4.4× the AUC of standardized extract at equal silybin. That is the basis of Nutramax's "up to 4 times" wording (AUC 11,200 vs 2,520 = 4.4×).

**Supplier:** Denamarin's SPC is very likely the Indena-type phytosome. "Phytosome" is Indena's term, used by the Nutramax-authored dog study, and Denamarin's silybin/SPC ratios match about 30% silybin phytosome material. Nutramax never names the supplier on the pages I accessed.

### Gaps
- **Supplier:** Nutramax does not state that Denamarin's SPC is Indena Siliphos/IdB 1016. The link is inferred.
- **Siliphos specification:** I did not obtain an Indena technical sheet giving Siliphos's exact silybin % or silybin:phosphatidylcholine ratio. Secondary sources conflict (1:1 vs 1:2 molar; "~30%" or "29.7–36.3%" silybin), so these were not used as facts.
- **Measurement method:** commercial "80% silymarin" claims are often UV-spectrophotometric and can overstate HPLC-measured silybin. I saw this only in supplier search snippets (not verified).
- Marin's vitamin E and zinc amounts come only from search snippets of a blocked page.
