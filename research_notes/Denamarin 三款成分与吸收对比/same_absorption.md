# SAMe oral absorption in dogs, and how it differs among Denamarin coated, Denamarin Chewable and Denamarin Advanced

Evidence tags used below: [PR] peer-reviewed journal; [CA] conference abstract or proceedings; [PAT] patent; [MFR] manufacturer label, marketing or "data on file" (unpublished); [DIST] distributor/retailer copy of manufacturer claims; [HUM] human data (reference only); [CAT]/[RAT] other species; [SNIP] wording seen only in a search-engine snippet because the page blocked direct fetching (lower confidence); [EXTRAP] my own extrapolation/calculation. All research done 2026-11-09.

## Q1. Peer-reviewed (or other independent) pharmacokinetic data for oral SAMe in dogs

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed study gives an absolute oral bioavailability (%) for SAMe in dogs; I found no dog IV-versus-oral SAMe study at all. The only peer-reviewed dog study (Center et al. 2005, AJVR) says only that an enteric-coated 1,4-butanedisulfonate SAMe salt (Nutramax Denosyl-SD4) was "biologically available" at 20 mg/kg/day. The label line "peak plasma levels in most dogs within 4 hours" is Nutramax's citation of that paper. Numeric dog Cmax values exist only in Nutramax marketing (Q2) and in a non-Nutramax patent whose dog numbers appear only in figures.

### Cited Findings
- **Center et al. 2005, AJVR 66(2):330-341 [PR]:** 12 healthy dogs, a 4-dog pilot then 2 crossover groups of 4. Quote: "To evaluate the influence of a 1,4-butanedisulfonate stable salt of S-adenosylmethionine (SAMe) administered orally…". Dose: "SAMe (20 mg/kg/d divided in 2 doses)". Result: "The stable salt of SAMe was biologically available; plasma concentrations of SAMe or prednisolone were not affected by coadministration." The abstract gives no Cmax, Tmax, AUC, half-life or bioavailability % — [PubMed 15757136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15757136/), doi:10.2460/ajvr.2005.66.330.
- **Nutramax labels cite that dog study for Tmax [MFR, citing PR]:**
  - Denosyl enteric-coated insert: "In a pharmacokinetic study with fasted dogs given Denosyl, peak plasma levels were recorded in most dogs within 4 hours of administration.1" Ref 1 is Center SA et al., Am J Vet Res 2005;66(2):330-341 — [Denosyl package insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denosyl.pdf).
  - Denamarin coated-tablet insert (2009 PDF) has the same sentence with "S-Adenosylmethionine" in place of "Denosyl" — [Denamarin package insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denamarin.pdf).
- **Nutramax research handout (2009) [MFR]:** "Results of a pilot study in dogs showed that bioavailability and pharmacokinetics of Denosyl and prednisolone were not affected by administering the two compounds together." — [Nutramax Denosyl research summary](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/Nutramax-Denosyl.pdf).
- **The product in Center's studies [CA]:** Denosyl-SD4, a stabilized 1,4-butanedisulfonate SAMe salt in enteric-coated tablets made by Nutramax Laboratories. This is paraphrased by the fetch tool, not quoted — [Warner & Center, WSAVA 2002 abstract on VIN](https://www.vin.com/apputil/content/defaultadv1.aspx?pId=11147&catId=29550&id=3846407).
- **Stramentinoli 1987, Am J Med 83(5A):35-42 [PR review, BioResearch S.p.A.]:** The abstract has no numbers by species. Quote: "data are reported on plasma kinetics, distribution, and metabolism of SAMe after oral administration… The results demonstrate the intestinal absorption of SAMe and its active metabolism." — [PubMed 3318439](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3318439/), doi:10.1016/0002-9343(87)90849-7.
- **Stramentinoli, Gualano & Galli-Kienle 1979, J Pharmacol Exp Ther 209:323-326 [PR; radiolabel work, species not stated in the abstract]:** "When [methyl-14C]SAMe is given orally, the radioactivity found in the liver is associated both with SAMe and phosphatidylcholine… These results and low plasma levels after the oral administration may be suggestive of a first-pass effect of SAMe characterized by an extensive uptake of the drug by the liver where it is rapidly metabolized." — [PubMed 439007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/439007/).
- **Stramentinoli & Catto 1976, "Pharmacokinetic studies of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAMe) in several animal species," Pharmacol Res Commun 8(2):211-218 [PR]:** PubMed has no abstract, and I could not reach the full text — [PubMed 1028081](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1028081/).
- **Endogenous plasma SAMe baseline [PR]:** measured by radioenzymatic assay, plasma "concentrations have been found to range between 17 and 72 ng/ml in the different animal species." — [Giulidori & Stramentinoli 1984, Anal Biochem, PubMed 6731799](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6731799/).
- **Dog acetaminophen case report [PR, efficacy only]:** "A loading dose (40 mg/kg body weight) of a stable SAMe salt per os was followed by a maintenance dose (20 mg/kg body weight) sid for 7 days"; RBC GSH responded "within 72 hours". No PK data — [Wallace et al. 2002 JAAHA, PubMed 12022411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12022411/).
- **Beagle PK in a patent [PAT; MSI Methylation Sciences, not Nutramax]:**
  - Design: "fasted male beagle dogs (7-10 kg)… Each group (consisting of 6 dogs) was dosed with a single orally administered enteric coated tablet, under fasted conditions". Tablets held 400 mg SAMe ion (from SAMe disulfate tosylate) with or without 25 mg of a gallate. Comparators included "a commercially available SAMe product (400 mg)". Sampling ran to 8 h, assayed by LC-MS/MS.
  - The Cmax and AUC results appear only in figures; the text gives no numbers — [US 12,303,524 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12303524B2/en).
- **Cat comparison, same Nutramax salt, the closest numeric PK [CA, CAT]:**
  - Design: 15 cats given Denosyl-SD4 enteric-coated tablets at mean 53.8 mg/kg, after a 12-h fast with food withheld 4 h more.
  - Plasma SAMe on Day 0 (mean ± SD, ng/mL): baseline 39.4±10.6; 2 h 1,432±270; 4 h 1,482±284; 8 h 895±158; 12 h 473±139.
  - Day 0 AUC was 11,877±9,356 (units not stated in the VIN summary). Peaks fell at 2–4 h, and no bioavailability % was reported — [WSAVA 2002 abstract (VIN)](https://www.vin.com/apputil/content/defaultadv1.aspx?pId=11147&catId=29550&id=3846407).
- **Peer-reviewed version of the cat study [PR, CAT]:** "enteric coated tablets providing 180 mg SAMe), dosage 48 mg/kg PO q24h, on an empty stomach… peak concentrations usually occurred 2-4 hours after dosing. Chronic SAMe administration did not change peak or cumulative plasma SAMe concentrations" — [Center et al. 2005 JVIM 19:303-314, PubMed 15954543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15954543/).
- **Product content varies [PR]:** 64 samples from 5 manufacturers showed "greater than threefold variation in the percentage of measured S-adenosyl methionine compared to the stated amount on the packaging". The abstract does not name the brands — [Sturgess 2014 JSAP, PubMed 25040219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25040219/).

### Inferences
- The absolute systemic bioavailability of SAMe in dogs is unknown. Any "% absorbed" for a dog has to be borrowed from human data (Q6) and labelled as an extrapolation.
- The cat AUC SD/mean of about 79% (9,356/11,877) shows that enteric-coated SAMe tablets vary greatly between animals. Dogs are probably similar, but I found no dog numbers to confirm this.
- The original Nutramax rule of 20 mg/kg/day (Denosyl insert: "Daily administration may also be calculated based on 20 mg/kg of body weight") matches the dose used in the Center 2005 dog study.
- The 1979 rat/radiolabel work shows strong first-pass hepatic uptake. A low plasma (systemic) bioavailability therefore understates how much SAMe the liver itself receives, and the liver is the target organ.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full text of Center 2005 AJVR: the AVMA journal site returned HTTP 403. Any plasma SAMe numbers from the dog pilot (Cmax, Tmax, AUC, variability) remain unverified.
- The full texts of Stramentinoli 1987 (Elsevier) and Stramentinoli & Catto 1976 were not accessible, and no secondary source I found quotes a dog oral bioavailability % from them.
- The Webster & Cooper 2009 VCNA review and the Plumb's SAMe monograph were not accessible: the search engine did not return the Plumb's text, and the theclinics.com PDF returned 403.
- I found no dog elimination half-life after oral SAMe and no dog IV data.

## Q2. Nutramax-sponsored PK comparisons: verifying claims (1)–(4) about coated vs chewable vs Advanced

### Takeaway
All four claims come from Nutramax's own unpublished data. I found no peer-reviewed paper, ACVIM/JVIM abstract or white paper with methods.
- **Claim (1), chewable vs coated: confirmed in words only.** A 2008 "data on file" study in fasted dogs compared a SAMe chewable with an enteric-coated SAMe tablet. Bioavailability was "comparable", and uptake was "significantly more rapid and consistent" with the chewable. No AUC, Tmax or variability numbers were ever published. The study compared SAMe-only (Denosyl-type) tablets, not Denamarin itself.
- **Claim (2), about 4x Cmax: roughly confirmed.** A 2017 Nutramax brochure chart shows canine plasma SAMe Cmax of about 1,250 ng/mL for "Denamarin" vs about 5,450 ng/mL for Advanced "when used at the same dose", about 4.3x. These are my pixel readings of a bar chart, and the dose is not stated.
- **Claim (3), 8 vs 20 mg/kg: confirmed.** The brochure text says 8 mg/kg/day of NMXSS75A "provided comparable CMAX levels" to NMXSS75 at 20 mg/kg/day; the chart reads about 1,240 vs 1,290 ng/mL.
- **Claim (4), unpublished: confirmed.** The data are Cmax only. No AUC, Tmax, n, error type, feeding state or tablet form is disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- **Claim (1), Denamarin Chewable package insert (PDF created Feb 2009) [MFR]:** "In a pharmacokinetic study with fasted dogs given a SAMe chewable tablet versus an enteric coated SAMe tablet, bioavailability was found to be comparable between the two formulations. The time course of uptake, however, was significantly more rapid and consistent with the chewable tablet compared to the enteric coated SAMe tablet." Its reference list includes "24. Data on file. Nutramax Laboratories, Inc., Edgewood, MD 21040, USA 2008." — [Denamarin Chewable insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-DenamarinChew.pdf).
- **Claim (1), Denosyl Chewable insert [MFR]:** the same statement, about the "Denosyl®" enteric-coated tablet, is footnoted to "11. Data on file. Nutramax Laboratories, Inc., Edgewood, MD 21040, USA 2008." — [Denosyl Chewable insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-DenosylChew.pdf).
- **Claims (2)–(4), 2017 Nutramax brochure [MFR]:** document code 03.1069.00 / LV-5281, PDF created 20 Sep 2017, hosted by Norwegian pharmacy VESO — [Nutramax "A New Day is Dawning on Liver Health" brochure (PDF)](https://res.cloudinary.com/veso-apotek-no/image/upload/v1578478990/p_859842_documentation_1).
  - Text, panel 1: "Initial pharmacokinetic research showed that the NMXSS75A™ S-Adenosylmethionine in Denamarin Advanced has greater bioavailability compared to the NMXSS75® S-Adenosylmethionine when used at the same dose."
  - Text, panel 2: "Further research showed that administration of the NMXSS75A™ S-Adenosylmethionine at a reduced level of 8 mg/kg/day provided comparable CMAX levels as administration of NMXSS75® S-Adenosylmethionine at 20 mg/kg/day."
  - Chart 1, "Canine Plasma SAMe Cmax" at the same dose, in ng/mL (my pixel measurement, ±about 25 ng/mL): Denamarin ≈1,256 with upper error bar ≈1,618; Advanced ≈5,464 with upper error bar ≈7,749. Ratio ≈4.35.
  - Chart 2, in ng/mL: Denamarin at 20 mg/kg ≈1,292 with upper error bar ≈1,794; Advanced at 8 mg/kg ≈1,238 with upper error bar ≈1,835.
  - The brochure does not say whether the error bars are SD or SEM, and gives no n, no feeding state and no AUC or Tmax.
  - Other brochure claims: "Quicker and more consistent SAMe absorption than enteric-coated tablets." and "Proprietary NMXSS75A™ SAMe salt: • Greater bioavailability Lower administration of 8 mg/kg/day compared to original Denamarin at 20 mg/kg/day".
- **Nutramax sales "SoundByte" [MFR]:** "Proprietary NMXSS75ATM SAMe salt… Studies show that canine Cmax blood level concentrations were higher with Denamarin Advanced versus Denamarin." Its comparison table lists original chewable 225 mg vs Advanced 120 mg (Small–Med) and 215 mg (Large) SAMe per tablet — [Vet Advantage-hosted PDF](https://vet-advantage.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2019/10/Nutramax-Denamarin-Advanced-SoundByte.pdf).
- **PennVet distributor listing [DIST][SNIP]:** "Utilizing the latest SAMe innovation, our propriety NMXSS75A™ SAMe salt has better bioavailability compared to original Denamarin® NMXSS75® and is therefore absorbed quicker." and "Lower administration levels at 8 mg/kg/day compared to original Denamarin® at 20 mg/kg/day". Direct fetch returned 403 — [PennVet medium-dog listing](https://www.pennvet.com/customer/portal/catalog/home/productdetail/nmx1380/denamarin+adv+hard+tab+med+dog+dispensing+carton+-+4x30ct); [PennVet cat/small listing](https://www.pennvet.com/customer/portal/catalog/home/productdetail/nmx1370/denamarin+adv+hard+tab+cat_sm+dispensing+carton+-+4x30ct).
- **Launch press, 3 Oct 2017 [MFR via trade press]:** "The pure, stabilized salt used in Denamarin Advanced 'provides greater absorption compared to original Denamarin salt,' the company added." — [Today's Veterinary Business](https://todaysveterinarybusiness.com/denamarin-advanced-limited-veterinary-channel/).
- **Protexin (UK distributor) detailer for the yeast-SAMe version [MFR]:** "Consequently, lower amounts of SAMe reach the same area under the curve (AUC) as traditional SAMe compounds.12". Reference 12 is "Data on file – Nutramax Laboratories" — [Protexin detailer PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/protexin-detailer-denamarin.pdf); [Protexin reference list](https://www.protexinvet.com/den-refs/).
- **Covetrus distributor page [DIST]:** "Greater bioavailability – NMXSS75A® SAMe in Denamarin® Advanced has greater bioavailability than original Denamarin" — [Covetrus Advanced Chewable S/M](https://www.northamerica.covetrus.com/Product/printdetails?sku=061007); [Covetrus Advanced Hard Cats/Small Dogs](https://northamerica.covetrus.com/Product/printdetails?sku=070824).
- **Current Nutramax Advanced Chewable page [MFR]:** "Greater bioavailability than original Denamarin" — [denamarin.com Advanced Chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/advanced-chewable-tablets).

### Inferences
- **Variability claim not supported for Advanced.** I read the error bars as a share of the mean. Original: about 29% (same-dose chart) and about 39% (20 mg/kg chart). Advanced: about 42% and about 48%. Nothing in the chart shows Advanced lowering dog-to-dog variability in Cmax. "More consistent" in Nutramax copy refers to the time course of absorption, not Cmax spread.
- **Comparator is probably the chewable, not the coated tablet.** The brochure's "original" is NMXSS75 SAMe, the specification named on the chewable labels (Q5). So the 4x and 8-vs-20 mg/kg comparisons are most likely Advanced vs the original chewable, and the brochure does not show they apply to the enteric-coated tablet. This is uncertain; the brochure does not say which tablet form was used.
- **The two charts are internally inconsistent.** The "same-dose" Denamarin bar (≈1,256) nearly equals the 20 mg/kg bar (≈1,292), which hints that the same-dose test was at 20 mg/kg. If so, Advanced would give ≈5,460 ng/mL at 20 mg/kg but ≈1,240 at 8 mg/kg: 2.5x the dose producing 4.4x the Cmax. That means either supra-proportional absorption or separate studies with different dogs. The brochure cannot resolve this.
- **Cmax is not the amount absorbed.** A faster-absorbing product raises Cmax even when total absorption (AUC) is equal. The size of the Advanced advantage in amount absorbed is therefore unknown, with plausible per-mg factors spanning:
  - about 1x, if only the absorption rate differs;
  - about 2.4x, implied by the label's 8 vs 20 mg/kg Cmax equivalence: (20/8) × (1,238/1,292);
  - about 4.3x, from the same-dose Cmax ratio.
  Protexin's "same AUC with lower amounts" is the only AUC-level claim, and it has no numbers.

### Gaps
- I found no ACVIM Forum/JVIM abstract, peer-reviewed paper, white paper or technical bulletin with methods or numbers for any of these comparisons. Searches covered the web, PubMed, distributor sites and Google Patents.
- For the 2008 chewable-vs-enteric study, none of these are available: the AUC ratio, Tmax values, CV/SD, dog count, dose, or which tablets were used.
- For the Advanced study, none of these are available: AUC, Tmax, n, error-bar type, fasting state, chewable or hard tablet, or the dose behind the "same dose" chart.
- I found no comparison of Advanced vs the enteric-coated original reported separately from the chewable.

## Q3. What SAMe technology does Denamarin Advanced use? Can it be given with food? Is there food-effect data?

### Takeaway
- **US product.** Nutramax calls the SAMe "NMXSS75A", a "proprietary SAMe salt" or "proprietary veterinary researched specification", and does not publicly disclose its chemistry. The hard tablets are not described as enteric-coated, and one distributor quotes "enteric coating is no longer required".
- **Patents.** I found no patent on the NMXSS75A salt. The patents printed on the Advanced brochure are US 6,555,141, 6,863,906 and 7,563,779. The one I could open (6,555,141) is a 1998 Nutramax patent on SAMe with milk thistle (plus L-ergothioneine), not a salt or delivery technology.
- **UK version.** The UK Protexin-distributed "Denamarin Advanced for Dogs" lists "SAMe found in inactivated yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae)" at 59 mg or 161 mg per tablet, different strengths from the US product. It therefore cannot be assumed to be the same material as US NMXSS75A.
- **Food.** Both US Advanced labels say "empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption", but add that it "may be administered with a small amount of food" if pilling is impossible. I found no food-effect data for Advanced.

### Inferences
- **US NMXSS75A may or may not be yeast-derived SAMe.** The UK "yeast SAMe" description, the ≥90% S,S isomer claim and the cited Gnosis yeast patent fit Advanced's claimed stability and bioavailability gains, and fit dropping the enteric coat. However, US labels call NMXSS75A a "salt", strengths differ (US 120/215 mg chewable vs UK 59/161 mg), and no US source mentions yeast. Treat the US product as an undisclosed proprietary SAMe.
- **Food allowance is a practical concession, not evidence.** The Advanced labels' small-food allowance is a pilling convenience. Nutramax still says "empty stomach… for optimal absorption", so no food-effect magnitude is implied.

### Gaps
- I found no disclosure of NMXSS75A's chemistry (counter-ion, isomer ratio, yeast or synthetic). I found no Nutramax patent on SAMe salt or delivery: Google Patents returned errors for US 6,863,906 and 7,563,779, so I could not confirm their subject.
- I found no fed-vs-fasted data for Denamarin Advanced in dogs.

## Q4. Food effect in dogs for regular Denamarin: the basis for "give on an empty stomach, at least 1 hour before food"

### Takeaway
No dog study quantifying how much food reduces SAMe absorption was found. The label wording ("the presence of food decreases the absorption of S-Adenosylmethionine") traces to a 2000 ACVIM proceedings lecture by Sharon Center, not a published fed/fasted study. The dog and cat PK studies dosed fasted animals, so they do not measure a food effect. Human food-effect data conflict:
- MSI-195 with a high-fat meal: AUC fell to about 55% of fasted.
- A conventional ademetionine 1,4-butanedisulfonate enteric tablet: AUC about 26% higher when fed, but Tmax was delayed from 4.5 to 7.5 h.

### Cited Findings
- **Denamarin coated insert (2009) [MFR]:** "For optimal absorption, the tablets should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before feeding, as the presence of food decreases the absorption of S-Adenosylmethionine." No magnitude or reference is attached — [Denamarin insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denamarin.pdf).
- **Denosyl research handout [MFR]:** "The presence of food decreases optimal absorption of S-Adenosylmethionine; it is, therefore, recommended to administer Denosyl on an empty stomach.1" Reference 1 is "Center SA. S-adenosyl-methionine an antioxidant and anti-inflammatory nutraceutical. Proceedings of the 18th ACVIM, Seattle, WA:550-552, 2000." [CA/proceedings] — [Nutramax Denosyl handout](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/Nutramax-Denosyl.pdf).
- **Denosyl enteric-coated insert [MFR]:** after the empty-stomach advice it adds, "For those owners who have difficulty administering tablets to their pets, Denosyl® tablet(s) may be disguised in a small bite of food." — [Denosyl insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denosyl.pdf).
- **Current Nutramax pages [MFR]:** Coated: "Denamarin Coated Tablets should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption." Chewable: same wording — [Coated](https://www.denamarin.com/coated-tablets); [Chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/chewable-tablets).
- **Fasting protocols in the PK studies [CA, CAT; MFR]:** the cats were dosed after a 12-hour fast, with food withheld for 4 more hours (paraphrase of the VIN abstract); the dog Tmax statement refers to "fasted dogs" — [WSAVA 2002 abstract](https://www.vin.com/apputil/content/defaultadv1.aspx?pId=11147&catId=29550&id=3846407); [Denosyl insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denosyl.pdf).
- **Veterinary proceedings, Susan E. Johnson, 2010 [CA]:** "Giving the drug on an empty stomach will also improve absorption." No data — [dvm360, Update on hepatoprotective therapies](https://www.dvm360.com/view/update-hepatoprotective-therapies-proceedings).
- **Veterinary proceedings [CA][SNIP]:** a search summary of another dvm360 proceedings piece described the veterinary SAMe product as an enteric-coated tablet with confirmed bioavailability when given on an empty stomach "(followed by a 2-hr fast)", with maximal plasma concentrations "within 4 to 8 hrs". Not fetched or verified verbatim — [dvm360, Treatment of hepatobiliary disease in dogs and cats](https://www.dvm360.com/view/treatment-hepatobiliary-disease-dogs-and-cats).
- **Human food effect, MSI-195 enteric tablet with propyl gallate, 800 mg [HUM][PR]:**
  - Tmax went "from 4.5 h under fasted conditions to 13 h under fed conditions, and area under the curve with food reduced to 55% of that seen under fasting conditions."
  - Cmax was 3,280.8 ng/mL fasted vs 1,461.0 ng/mL fed; AUCT was 9,434.4 vs 5,224.7 ng·h/mL. The meal was high-fat, high-calorie, 30 min before dosing.
  - Sources: [Cameron et al. 2020 BMC Pharmacol Toxicol 21:88 (PMC7734772)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/), doi:10.1186/s40360-020-00466-7.
- **Human food effect, conventional ademetionine 1,4-butanedisulfonate enteric tablet [HUM][PR]:** 24 subjects, single dose. Tmax "4.50 ± 1.07 and 7.50 ± 1.58 for the fasting and postprandial groups"; "higher exposure in the postprandial group (AUC0-inf, 4021.02 ± 3377.13 and 5087.28 ± 3539.26 for the fasting and postprandial groups)" — [Zhang et al. 2022 J Clin Pharm Ther, PubMed 34981547](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34981547/), doi:10.1111/jcpt.13601.
- **Human label-style text [HUM; SmPC-derived summary]:** "Oral bioavailability is enhanced when ademetionine is administered under fasting conditions." — [RxReasoner ademetionine pharmacology](https://www.rxreasoner.com/substances/ademetionine/pharmacology).

### Inferences
- The dog empty-stomach instruction is reasonable but unquantified for dogs. In humans the effect of food ranges from about −45% AUC (high-fat meal, enhanced formulation) to about +26% AUC (conventional enteric tablet), with a consistent delay in Tmax. My computed SD/mean ratios are about 70–84% in the Zhang study.
- **Proposed mechanism (my inference).** A large non-disintegrating enteric-coated tablet must leave the stomach before it can dissolve, and food delays and randomises gastric emptying. MSI-195's authors describe this for humans: "The Tmax for enteric coated oral dosage forms is highly dependant on gastric emptying…" ([PMC7734772](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/)). The same mechanism plausibly applies to dogs. If so, food would hit the enteric-coated tablet harder than a chewable that disintegrates in the stomach, but no dog data confirm this.

### Gaps
- No dog fed-vs-fasted SAMe PK data were found, so there is no magnitude for the reduction with food in dogs.
- The Center 2000 ACVIM proceedings text, the cited basis for the food statement, was not accessible.
- No food-effect data were found for the chewable or Advanced.

## Q5. Enteric coating: is SAMe destroyed by gastric acid? What happens if the coated tablet is chewed, split or crushed? How does the chewable protect SAMe?

### Takeaway
- **Acid is not the main threat.** SAMe's documented chemical instability is at neutral or alkaline pH, where it cleaves to MTA and homoserine lactone, and analysts acidify plasma to stabilise it. I found no primary evidence that gastric acid destroys SAMe. The "inactivation in gastric acid" rationale appears in veterinary proceedings without data.
- **What the coating is for.** Moisture is handled by blister or desiccated packaging. In human formulations the coating is credited with tolerability and with "bioavailability".
- **The coating does not seem needed for absorption in dogs.** Nutramax's own claims say the non-enteric chewable had comparable bioavailability with faster, more consistent uptake, and that Advanced dropped the enteric coat.
- **No data on broken tablets.** I found no data on absorption after chewing, splitting or crushing an enteric-coated SAMe tablet in dogs; labels simply forbid it.

### Cited Findings
- **Chemistry [PR review]:**
  - "Under physiological conditions, SAM can undergo a non-enzymatic cleavage reaction and degrade into homoserine and MTA." and "SAM is markedly unstable at pH 7.5 and can rapidly degrade into 5'-methylthioadenosine (MTA) through a non-enzymatic cleavage reaction".
  - Stable salts: "Efforts towards making more stable salts of SAM, such as the widely used sulfate and p-toluenesulfonate double salts of SAM, have yielded improved dry-state stability".
  - Intestinal transport: "the apparent permeability coefficient of SAM (0.6×10-6 to 0.7×10-6 cm/s) is much lower than the typical value for passive diffusion… the main mode of SAM transport is paracellular".
  - Source: [Sun & Locasale 2021, Cell Stress (PMC8728568)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8728568/), doi:10.15698/cst2022.01.261.
- **Assay practice [HUM][PR]:** human plasma was transferred into tubes "containing 40 μL of a solution of 10% H3PO4, for analyte stability" — [Cameron et al. 2020 (PMC7734772)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/).
- **Human formulation rationale [HUM][PR]:** "Current oral formulations are enteric coated, which helps with tolerability and bioavailability however, even with that, oral bioavailability is low." — [Cameron et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/).
- **Generic coating rationale [PAT]:** "protect the active ingredient from the harsh environment of the stomach" and "SAMe and other tablets described in the art are commonly enteric coated." The patent states no acid-degradation data — [MSI patent US8329208B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8329208B2/en).
- **Veterinary proceedings (Johnson, 2010) [CA]:** "The enteric coating is necessary to prevent inactivation in gastric acid." and "Consequently, the enteric-coated tablets should not be broken or crushed." Also: "Both are formulated as patented stabilized salts, in foil-wrapped packets (because the salts are hydroscopic)." — [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/update-hepatoprotective-therapies-proceedings). This contradicts the pH-stability chemistry above and cites no data.
- **Nutramax handling instructions [MFR]:**
  - FAQ: "Denamarin enteric-coated tablets should generally not be broken. When increasing or decreasing their administration, adjustments should be done in full tablet increments." — [denamarin.com FAQ](https://www.denamarin.com/frequently-asked-questions-about-denamarin-liver-health-supplements).
  - Denosyl insert: "Tablets are sensitive to moisture and extreme heat and should not be split or crumbled." — [Denosyl insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denosyl.pdf).
  - Denamarin insert: "As S-Adenosylmethionine is sensitive to moisture, the enteric-coated tablets are individually sealed in specialized moisture-resistant blister packs." — [Denamarin insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-Denamarin.pdf).
- **How the chewable protects SAMe [MFR]:**
  - "While S-Adenosylmethionine is sensitive to moisture, the specific NMXSS75™ S-Adenosylmethionine found in Denamarin chewable tablets has been manufactured to maintain stability."
  - Storage: "Keep bottle closed" with desiccant, per the parallel Denosyl Chewable wording "Keep bottle closed with desiccant inside when not removing tablets."
  - The chewable is scored and dosed down to ¼ tablet.
  - Sources: [Denamarin Chewable insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-DenamarinChew.pdf); [Denosyl Chewable insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-DenosylChew.pdf); [denamarin.com Chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/chewable-tablets).
- **Non-enteric chewable vs enteric tablet in dogs [MFR, unpublished 2008]:** "bioavailability was found to be comparable… uptake… significantly more rapid and consistent with the chewable tablet" — [Denamarin Chewable insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-DenamarinChew.pdf).
- **Advanced [MFR][DIST][SNIP]:** "Quicker and more consistent SAMe absorption than enteric-coated tablets." ([Nutramax brochure](https://res.cloudinary.com/veso-apotek-no/image/upload/v1578478990/p_859842_documentation_1)); "enteric coating is no longer required" ([Banfield, snippet](https://shop.banfield.com/denamarin-advanced-tablets-cats-dogs.html)).
- **Salt form changes PK [RAT][PR; Gnosis-sponsored]:** a SAMe phytate salt "displayed significant better pharmacokinetic parameters compared to SAM PTS", and the "phytate anion protects SAM from degradation" — [Francioso et al. 2021 Amino Acids, PubMed 34536129](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34536129/).
- **Unsupported consumer claim:** "Food triggers stomach acid production that can degrade SAMe before it reaches the intestine" (no source given) — [Bestie Paws](https://www.bestiepaws.com/dog-supplement-review/denamarin-advanced/). This contradicts the chemistry above.

### Inferences
- **What the coat is really for.** The best-supported reasons are (a) human GI tolerability, (b) delivering intact SAMe to the upper small intestine, and (c) keeping a moisture-sensitive core sealed. Protection from acid hydrolysis is not established.
- **Splitting a coated tablet is unlikely to destroy the SAMe.** The likelier consequences are moisture or air exposure of the core and loss of dose accuracy. Splitting would effectively convert the tablet into a non-enteric form. Nutramax's chewable data suggest a non-enteric form can absorb comparably or faster in dogs. However, the coated tablet's core salt is not the stabilised chewable specification, and no data exist on how fast that core degrades once broken. **This is inference only.** The label instruction not to split stands, and no dog data exist on crushed coated tablets.
- **The chewable's protection is formulation, not coating.** It relies on a stabilised SAMe specification plus moisture-controlled packaging.

### Gaps
- I found no primary study measuring SAMe degradation in simulated gastric fluid at pH 1–3 at 37 °C. The search surfaced pH-rate kinetic papers that I did not verify directly.
- I found no dog or cat data on absorption after crushing, splitting or chewing an enteric-coated SAMe tablet.

## Q6. Human reference data (labelled HUMAN): oral bioavailability of conventional enteric-coated SAMe and the effect of food

### Takeaway
The best human estimate of the oral bioavailability of conventional enteric-coated SAMe is 2.14–2.60%. This comes from single-dose oral:IV AUC0-24 ratios in women (2.14%) and men (2.60%) given 1,000 mg SAMe tosylate disulfate after an overnight fast (Yang et al. 2009, Clin Ther, n=5 per sex per route).
- **The "2.1–2.6% fasted" figure is from this paper.** Both numbers are fasted; the range is men vs women, not fasted vs fed. One sentence in the MSI-195 paper mislabels them as single- vs multiple-dose.
- **Peak levels.** Human peak plasma levels are 0.5–1 mg/L at 3–5 h after 400–1,000 mg enteric-coated tablets.
- **Absorbed vs intact.** Radiolabel data suggest most of the oral dose's methyl label is absorbed or retained in some form, even though intact-SAMe systemic bioavailability is only about 2%.
- **Food.** The effect of food in humans is inconsistent across formulations (see Q4).

### Cited Findings
- **Yang et al. 2009, Clin Ther 31(2):311-320 [HUM][PR]:**
  - Design: "STD was administered as single and multiple doses of enteric-coated tablets and IV infusion… After an overnight fast, male and female Chinese volunteers were assigned to receive STD 1000 mg for 5 days, either in enteric-coated tablet formulation or as a 250-mL IV infusion… For the oral formulation, SAMe concentrations were corrected for concentrations of endogenous SAMe."
  - Oral single dose: "C(max), 2.37 (1.58) and 2.50 (1.83) micromol/L; T(max), 5.40 (1.14) and 5.20 (1.48) hours; AUC(0-24), 8.56 (5.16) and 10.3 (8.0) micromol/L/h".
  - Key ratio: "The single-dose oral:IV ratios of AUC(0-24) in men and women, respectively, were 2.60% and 2.14%".
  - Parallel groups of 5 men and 5 women per route.
  - Source: [PubMed 19302903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19302903/), doi:10.1016/j.clinthera.2009.02.010.
- **The MSI-195 paper quotes Yang twice, inconsistently [HUM][PR]:**
  - First: "Yang and co-workers determined an AUC0-24 Oral/IV ratio was determined to be 2.60% and 2.14% in men and women respectively [12]."
  - Later: "Yang and co-workers reported a oral to iv ratio of 2.6% and 2.14% for single and multiple oral dosing respectively [12]."
  - Source: [Cameron et al. 2020 (PMC7734772)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/).
- **MSI-195 vs a commercial product [HUM][PR, sponsor-authored]:**
  - Relative exposure: "On a per mg basis, MSI-195 under fasted conditions gave approximately threefold greater exposure than commercial SAMe as judged by Cmax (3.31-fold higher), AUCT (2.84-fold higher…". The comparator was Nature Made SAM-e Complete™ (Pharmavite), 1,600 mg fasted, with AUCT 6,643.8 ng·h/mL, or 4.15 ng·h/mL per mg vs 11.80 for MSI-195.
  - Absolute estimate: "the oral bioavailability of MSI-195 (based on 1600 mg in Stage 1) is approximately 9%. Clearly this is a very approximate assessment".
  - Variability: "PK parameters were generally quite variable".
  - Source: [Cameron et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/).
- **Human IV kinetics [HUM][PR]:** 100 and 500 mg IV; "terminal half-lives 81 +/- 8 and 101 +/- 7 min"; "Urinary excretion was 34 +/- 3 and 40 +/- 2% of the administered dose"; "Binding of AdoMet to serum proteins is negligible." — [Giulidori et al. 1984 Eur J Clin Pharmacol, PubMed 6489422](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6489422/).
- **Label-style human PK text [HUM; SmPC-derived summary]:**
  - Intramuscular: "When administered intramuscularly, absorption of ademetionine is practically complete (96%)".
  - Oral: "peak plasma concentrations of 0.5 to 1 mg/L achieved 3 to 5 hours after single doses ranging from 400 mg to 1000 mg"; "Oral bioavailability is enhanced when ademetionine is administered under fasting conditions."
  - Radiolabel: "In tracer balance studies using orally administered, radioactive (methyl 14C) SAMe in normal volunteers, urinary excretion of radioactivity was 15.5 ± 1.5% after 48 hours and fecal excretion was 23.5 ± 3.5% after 72 hours, leaving approximately 60% incorporated into stable pools."
  - Source: [RxReasoner ademetionine pharmacology](https://www.rxreasoner.com/substances/ademetionine/pharmacology).
- **Food effect in humans:** see Q4 for MSI-195 (AUC to 55% with a high-fat meal) and Zhang 2022 (AUC +26% fed, Tmax 4.5 to 7.5 h) — [PMC7734772](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7734772/); [PubMed 34981547](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34981547/).

### Inferences
- **Two meanings of "absorbed".** Intact SAMe reaching the systemic circulation is about 2–3% for conventional enteric tablets in fasted humans. The fraction of the oral dose that is absorbed in some form is much higher: the methyl-14C label had only about 23.5% fecal recovery at 72 h. The gap reflects degradation in the gut and lumen plus first-pass hepatic uptake and metabolism. For a liver supplement, hepatic delivery is likely higher than plasma bioavailability suggests.
- **Variability is large.** I computed SD/mean ratios of about 67–73% for Cmax and 60–78% for AUC in Yang 2009.
- **Rough cross-check.** If MSI-195 is about 9% and about 2.8x the commercial comparator, the commercial enteric SAMe would be about 3% (my calculation). That is broadly consistent with Yang's 2.1–2.6%.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain an official EMA or national SmPC document (Transmetil/Samyr/Heptral) with a stated oral bioavailability %. The RxReasoner text is an SmPC-style summary without a document ID.
- The Bottiglieri 2002 AJCN full text was not reachable (404) to check how it cites Stramentinoli 1987.
- A search summary said the Cell Stress 2021 review gives "0.58%–1.04%" oral bioavailability. I could not find these numbers in the fetched text, so treat them as unverified and do not use them.

## Q7. Converting to absorbed mg: comparison for a 9.1 kg (20 lb) dog

### Takeaway
Because there is no dog bioavailability %, "absorbed mg" can only be estimated by borrowing the human figure (intact SAMe 2.14–2.60% of dose) and applying Nutramax's unpublished relative claims. On that basis:
- **Regular coated 225 mg or chewable 225 mg:** about 4.8–5.9 mg of intact SAMe reaches the systemic blood per day. A wide band of 1–5% gives 2.3–11 mg.
- **Previous ½ Advanced chewable (60 mg):** about 1.3–1.6 mg with no advantage, about 3.1–3.7 mg with the 2.4x "8 vs 20 mg/kg" factor, or about 5.6–6.8 mg with the 4.35x same-dose Cmax factor. A wide band gives 0.6–13 mg.
- **By manufacturer Cmax claims alone,** ½ Advanced (6.6 mg/kg) is roughly equivalent to 144–261 mg of original SAMe, or 64–116% of one 225 mg tablet.

All of these are [EXTRAP] estimates with large uncertainty, not measured dog values.

### Cited Findings
- **Label amounts for a 20 lb dog [MFR]:**
  - Coated, medium dogs: "S-Adenosylmethionine 225 mg… 13 - 34 lbs (6 - 15.5 kg) 1" tablet/day — [denamarin.com Coated](https://www.denamarin.com/coated-tablets).
  - Chewable: "S-Adenosylmethionine 225 mg… 16-30 lbs 1" tablet/day — [denamarin.com Chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/chewable-tablets).
  - Advanced Chewable, small-medium: "S-Adenosylmethionine 120 mg… 15 - 25 lbs ½" tablet/day — [denamarin.com Advanced Chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/advanced-chewable-tablets).
  - Advanced Hard, medium dogs: "S-Adenosylmethionine 82 mg… 12 - 34 lbs 1" tablet/day — [denamarin.com Advanced Hard](https://www.denamarin.com/advanced-hard-tablets).
- **Human systemic bioavailability used for scaling [HUM][PR]:** 2.14% (women) to 2.60% (men), single dose, fasted, conventional enteric-coated tablet — [Yang 2009, PubMed 19302903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19302903/).
- **Relative dog factors [MFR, unpublished]:** chewable vs enteric "comparable" bioavailability ([Denamarin Chewable insert](https://wendyblount.com/handouts-drugs/PackageInsert-DenamarinChew.pdf)). For Advanced vs original, Cmax ≈5,464 vs ≈1,256 ng/mL at the same dose, and 8 mg/kg ≈1,238 vs 20 mg/kg ≈1,292 ng/mL, both my chart readings ([Nutramax brochure](https://res.cloudinary.com/veso-apotek-no/image/upload/v1578478990/p_859842_documentation_1)).

### Inferences

- **If "Advanced" meant the hard tablet.** For the Advanced Hard medium-dog tablet (82 mg), the label amount for 20 lb is 1 tablet (82 mg, 9.0 mg/kg). Half a tablet would be 41 mg (4.5 mg/kg), below the label amount. Estimates for 82 mg are 1.75–2.13 / 4.2–5.1 / 7.6–9.3 mg across the three scenarios; for 41 mg they are 0.9–1.1 / 2.1–2.6 / 3.8–4.6 mg. "Half a tablet" matches the label for a 20 lb dog only on the 120 mg small-medium chewable.
- **Cmax projection.** Scaling the brochure's dog Cmax values linearly, which is not verified and the charts suggest may not hold, gives about 1,600 ng/mL for 225 mg original (24.7 mg/kg) and about 1,020 ng/mL for 60 mg Advanced (6.6 mg/kg). Endogenous baseline is about 17–72 ng/mL across species.
- **The scenario choice dominates the estimate.** The width of the Advanced range comes mostly from the unknown relative factor, not from F. Peak-exposure equivalence (2.4x) is the manufacturer's own stated dosing basis (8 vs 20 mg/kg/day). Treat 2.4x as the manufacturer-implied central case, about 1x as the floor if Advanced only speeds absorption, and 4.35x as the upper case.
- **Caveats the report writer should repeat.**
  1. The F values are human data (n=5 per group, CV 60–80%); dog F is unmeasured.
  2. "Reaching blood as intact SAMe" understates hepatic delivery because of first-pass uptake.
  3. Label SAMe content may not match actual content; Sturgess 2014 found more than threefold variation across products, brands unnamed.
  4. All formulation comparisons are unpublished Nutramax Cmax data, apart from qualitative label statements.
