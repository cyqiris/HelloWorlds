# Oral absorption of silybin in dogs: the SPC in Denamarin vs plain silybin or silymarin

Evidence-level tags used below:
- **[PR]**: peer-reviewed paper.
- **[PR-ind]**: peer-reviewed, but written or funded by Nutramax.
- **[Letter]**: published correspondence.
- **[Mfr]**: manufacturer or distributor material, or a retailer copy of the label.
- **[Patent]**: patent text.
- **[Review]**: secondary source.
- **[Extrap]**: my own calculation or extrapolation (from another species, another dose, or another product).

Abbreviations:
- SPC: silybin–phosphatidylcholine complex.
- F: absolute oral bioavailability.
- AUC: area under the plasma concentration–time curve.

## Q1. Filburn, Kettenacker & Griffin 2007 (J Vet Pharmacol Ther 30:132–138): design, results, product, food

### Takeaway
This is the only canine SPC pharmacokinetic study, and Nutramax staff ran it. It used 8 beagles and a single dose of about 16 mg/kg silybin as SPC, roughly 7 times the label dose. The product was Marin, Nutramax's SPC plus vitamin E and zinc product, not Denamarin. The dose was apparently given after food.

Results for total plasma silybin:
- Cmax: 1,310 ng/mL.
- Tmax: 2.9 h.
- AUC0–24: 11,200 ng·h/mL.

Exposure was about 3 times that from a standardized silymarin extract at the same silybin dose (AUC ratio 3.0–4.4; Cmax ratio 2.8–2.9). The study gives **relative bioavailability only**. It has no IV arm and therefore no absolute bioavailability. It has no fed-versus-fasted comparison, and the abstract reports no half-life.

### Cited Findings
- **Verbatim abstract [PR-ind]:** "The potential for enhanced bioavailability of a phytosome complex containing phosphatidylcholine and silybin, the primary active flavonolignan in silymarin extract, was tested in dogs. A group of eight beagles (four males, four females) were dosed orally with a silybin-phosphatidylcholine complex (SPC) and a commercially available standardized silymarin extract containing equivalent levels of silybin. Dosing with the SPC resulted in Cmax, Tmax, and AUC0-24 h values (mean+/-SD) for total silybin of 1310+/-880 ng/mL, 2.87+/-2.23 h, and 11,200+/-6520 ng.h/mL, respectively; corresponding values for a standardized silymarin extract were 472+/-383 ng/mL, 4.75+/-2.82 h, and 3720+/-4970 ng.h/mL. A second, separate group of beagles were also dosed with the extract alone, yielding values of 449+/-402 ng/mL, 6.87+/-7.43 h, and 2520+/-2976 ng.h/mL. These data show that a phytosome complex of phosphatidylcholine and silybin markedly enhances bioavailability in dogs." — [PubMed 17348898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348898/); DOI [10.1111/j.1365-2885.2007.00834.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2885.2007.00834.x)
- **Author affiliation:** "Veterinary Science Division, Nutramax Laboratories, Inc., Edgewood, MD" — [PubMed 17348898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348898/)
- **Dose stated by the manufacturer [Mfr]:** The Denamarin vet detail aid (distributed by ADM/Protexin) reproduces the Filburn 2007 plasma curve. Its caption reads "Each given as one oral dose equivalent to 16mg/kg body weight of silybin." — [Denamarin vet detail aid PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/4028-denamarin_vet_detail_aid-lr-singles.pdf)
- **The product was Marin [Letter].** A 2013 JVIM letter says the following. Its signature block lists Griffin and Barnes (Nutramax) and Hackett and Twedt (Colorado State University); from the one-page PDF it is unclear whether the Colorado State University names are co-signers or the reviewers being addressed. The letter says "most of the referenced companion animal studies in this recent review on milk thistle used Marin, the earliest SPC product available to veterinarians." It continues: "In other studies evaluating the bioavailability of Marin, the plasma concentrations of silybin in dogs given SPC were 3–4.4 times greater than those in dogs given another oral silymarin extract." That sentence cites Filburn 2007. — [J Vet Intern Med 2013;27:755, Letter (doi 10.1111/jvim.12099)](https://academic.oup.com/jvim/article/27/4/755/8451857)
- **Background subtraction [Letter].** The letter's Figure 2 legend says the values are "the mean values ± standard deviation of measured levels after subtraction of background peaks averaged for days 1 and 0." — [JVIM 2013 Letter](https://academic.oup.com/jvim/article/27/4/755/8451857)
- **Companion safety study, same 8 beagles [PR-ind] (Filburn, Kettenacker & Griffin, Intern J Appl Res Vet Med 2006;4:326–334):**
  - Subjects: "Eight healthy, adult, purpose-bred beagles were used (4 males and 4 females), weighing 10.14-12.49 kg."
  - Day-1 dose: "On Day 1 of dosing, dogs received 178 mg silybin (A+B) equivalents complexed with phosphatidylcholine (SPC), 616 IU dα-tocopheryl acetate, and 118 mg elemental zinc as zinc gluconate (Marin®, Nutramax Laboratories, Inc.), along with 10 mL of water to assure timely entry into the stomach."
  - Sampling: "Blood samples were drawn 24 hours and immediately prior to dosing, then at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, and 24 hours for silybin pharmacokinetics, which are reported elsewhere (Griffin et al, submitted for publication)."
  - Result: "A pharmacokinetic study of plasma silybin levels in these same dogs ... showed an increase to a maximum of 1.31 ± 0.31 μg/mL at 3 hrs, followed by a return to background levels at 24 hrs, resulting in a mean area-under-the-curve value 300% to 400% that of standardized silymarin extract."
  - Assay: silybin (A+B) was measured by "B.T. Biotecnica, Varese, Italy."

  — [Filburn et al. 2006, IJARVM PDF](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Feeding state at dosing [PR-ind]:**
  - "Initial dosing at 118 mg of elemental zinc after a 23-hour fast resulted in vomiting. All dogs consumed this same level when administered on a full stomach with no vomiting... It is recommended to administer the supplement on a full stomach."
  - "Dosing following food intake was necessary at these higher levels for full acceptance of the supplement and avoidance of inappetence or emesis."

  — [Filburn et al. 2006](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Dose versus label [PR-ind]:** "The recommended administration for the size dogs used in this study is 105 IU vitamin E, 24 mg silybin, and 17 mg of zinc. Therefore, on Day 1 of the study, the dogs received 6 to 7 times the recommended maintenance administration levels." — [Filburn et al. 2006](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Comparison with other data [PR-ind]:** "The maximal concentration shown in dogs ... and bioavailability assessed as area-under-the-curve over 24 hours were comparable to those observed in humans and twice that shown in beagle dogs after adjusting for dose." The authors also wrote: "achieving substantial, albeit transient, levels in plasma requires daily dosing." — [Filburn et al. 2006](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Ratios [Extrap]:** These are my calculations from the 2007 abstract.

  | Comparison | Cmax ratio | AUC0–24 ratio |
  |---|---|---|
  | SPC vs extract, same group | 2.78 | 3.01 |
  | SPC vs extract, second group | 2.92 | 4.44 |

  Variability under SPC was large: the coefficient of variation was 67% for Cmax and 58% for AUC. For the extract, the SD of AUC exceeded the mean (3,720 ± 4,970). — computed from [PubMed 17348898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348898/)
- **Marketing claim that conflicts with the paper [Mfr]:** The detail aid says "studies show that peak plasma silybin levels are more than four times higher with a silybin/phosphatidylcholine complex (SPC) than silymarin alone." The published Cmax ratios are only 2.8–2.9; the 4.4 figure is an AUC ratio. — [Detail aid](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/4028-denamarin_vet_detail_aid-lr-singles.pdf) vs [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348898/)
- **Full text not reachable.** The Wiley full-text page returned HTTP 403, and the ResearchGate copy returned 403. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2885.2007.00834.x)

### Inferences
- **The SPC arm of the 2007 paper is almost certainly the Day-1 pharmacokinetics from the 2006 Marin study.** Five points match:
  1. Same dogs: 8 beagles, 4 males and 4 females.
  2. Same Cmax: 1.31 µg/mL.
  3. Matching spread: the 2007 SD of 880 equals the 2006 value of 0.31 × √8 = 0.877 µg/mL, so 0.31 is the SEM.
  4. The 2006 paper says the pharmacokinetics were "reported elsewhere (Griffin et al, submitted)."
  5. The dose matches: 178 mg per dog in 10.14–12.49 kg dogs is 14.3–17.6 mg/kg, consistent with the detail aid's "16 mg/kg".
- **The dose was very likely given in the fed state.** The 2006 paper says dosing at these levels required food, so the canine SPC data are best read as fed-state data. That is the opposite of the Denamarin label instruction. Confidence is moderate to high: the text is slightly ambiguous because an initial fasted attempt caused vomiting.
- **What was tested was Marin**, a different SPC product (SPC plus vitamin E plus zinc), not any Denamarin tablet. Denamarin's silybin claims are extrapolated from Marin data.
- **The relative benefit of SPC in dogs is about 3-fold.** It is 2.8–2.9 by Cmax and 3.0–4.4 by AUC, depending on which comparator group is used. Tmax was also earlier: 2.9 h vs 4.8–6.9 h.
- **Strength of the evidence:** peer-reviewed but written by industry, small sample, a single supra-label dose, a single dose occasion, and "total silybin" measured with background subtraction by a contract lab. The assay's hydrolysis method is unverified.

### Gaps
- Not verified because the full text was inaccessible:
  - whether the silymarin arm was a true crossover, and its washout and sequence;
  - whether the silymarin arm was also dosed fed;
  - the half-life;
  - individual-dog data;
  - the assay method (enzymatic hydrolysis or not).

  Where I looked: Wiley (403), ResearchGate (403), Unpaywall and Semantic Scholar (no open-access copy).
- **The abstract reports no absolute bioavailability, and the design has no IV arm.**
- The supplier of the SPC raw material is not disclosed in any source I found.

## Q2. Other canine studies of oral silybin or silymarin, and absolute oral bioavailability (dogs, then other species)

### Takeaway
**I found no absolute oral bioavailability value for silybin in dogs, for SPC or any other form.** The other canine pharmacokinetic studies are formulation papers, mostly from China, in fasted beagles or mongrels. They report only relative bioavailability against Legalon, silymarin, or silybin salts.

Absolute values exist only in other species:

| Species | Form | Absolute F | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat | Marin SPC, fasted | ≈7% | Webb 2012 |
| Horse | SPC in feed | 0.6% | Hackett 2013 |
| Horse | SPC by nasogastric tube | 2.9% | Hackett 2013 |
| Rat | plain silibinin | 0.73% | Wu 2007 |

I found no IV-referenced value for humans. Silipide's oral exposure is about 4.6 times that of silymarin (AUC), and only a few percent of the dose appears in urine.

### Cited Findings
- **Canine reviews with no absolute bioavailability.**
  - Hackett, Twedt & Gustafson 2013 [Review]: "Limited information currently is available regarding silibinin use in veterinary medicine. Future study is justified to evaluate dose, kinetics, and treatment effects in domestic animals." — [PubMed 23140176](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23140176/)
  - Tedesco & Guerrini 2023 [Review]: "The bioavailability of silybin was also studied in dogs, cats, and horses and this confirms a low bioavailability even if SIL was administered complexed with lipid molecules." — [Planta Med 2023, PMC11961297](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11961297/)
- **Dog formulation study with Legalon at 50 mg/kg [PR].** Six mongrel dogs (10 ± 0.2 kg), "fasted overnight", crossover with a 7-day washout, 50 mg/kg silymarin.

  | Formulation | Cmax (µg/mL) | Tmax (h) | AUC0–∞ (µg·h/mL) | t½ (h) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Legalon capsule | 1.06 ± 0.04 | 1.0 | 2.12 ± 0.07 | 1.71 |
  | SMEDDS | 1.85 | — | 5.23 | — |

  SMEDDS relative bioavailability was 227%. The paper gives no silybin-equivalent dose, and free vs total silybin is not stated. — [Li X et al. 2010, AAPS PharmSciTech, PMC2902333](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2902333/)
- **Other beagle formulation studies [PR]. All report relative bioavailability only (in their abstracts):**
  - Silymarin proliposome vs silymarin: Cmax 472.62 vs 89.78 ng/mL; AUC0–∞ 2,606.21 vs 697 ng·h/mL. — [Yan-yu 2006, PMID 16687221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16687221/)
  - Solid-dispersion pellets: about 5 times a Silybum marianum extract suspension. — [Sun 2008, PMID 18240100](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18240100/)
  - Liquid-crystalline matrix: 3.46 times Legalon. — [Lian 2011, PMID 21948306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21948306/)
  - Nanostructured lipid carrier: 2.54 times Legalon. — [Shangguan 2014, PMID 24008629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24008629/)
  - Mixed micelles: 252% of silybin-N-methylglucamine. — [Yu 2010, PMID 20523347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20523347/)
  - Nanocrystal tablet: about equal to a silybin meglumine tablet. — [Xu 2015, PMID 24495272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24495272/)
  - Nanosuspension, oral and IV in beagles: no absolute F in the abstract. — [Wang 2010, PMID 20332565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20332565/)
- **Dog plasma protein binding [PR]:** For silibinin hemisuccinate, "percentage binding ranging from 94.3% to 97.8%" across mouse, rat, dog and human plasma. — [Persiani 2013, PMID 23612650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23612650/)
- **Amanita poisoning in beagles [PR]:** an efficacy study, not pharmacokinetics. "Silibinin administration (50 mg/kg) 5 and 24 hr after intoxication suppressed the serum changes and the fall in prothrombin time... none of the silibinin-treated dogs died." — [Vogel et al. 1984, PMID 6719456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6719456/)
- **Cat, absolute F for Marin SPC [PR, non-MEDLINE journal; academic authors; funded by Winn Feline Foundation; SPC supplied by Nutramax].**
  - Cats were "held off food for 12 hours" and given "10 mg/kg of SPC as a powder combined with cornstarch (31% silibinin) in a capsule". "Each capsule delivered 40 mg of silibinin A+B." The IV comparator was silibinin 50 mg per cat.
  - Oral vs IV: Cmax "87.1µM ± 44.2 IV and 5.8µM ± 3.1 oral"; t½ "2.51hr ± 1.63 IV and 3.16hr ± 1.74 oral".
  - "Oral SPC has a bioavailability of approximately 7% in the cat."
  - "the bioavailability of SPC administered on an empty stomach in a capsule form to cats is between 6-7%."
  - Footnote: "Found in Marin®, Nutramax Laboratories, Inc."

  — [Webb, Gustafson & Twedt 2012, Intern J Appl Res Vet Med 10:107–112](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol10Iss2/Vol10%20Iss2Webb.pdf)
- **Horse, absolute F [PR]:** "Bioavailability of orally administered silibinin phospholipid was 0.6% PO in feed and 2.9% via nasogastric tube. During the multidose phase, silibinin had nonlinear pharmacokinetics." — [Hackett et al. 2013, Am J Vet Res, PMID 24066917](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24066917/)
- **Rat, absolute F of plain silibinin [PR]:** "The oral bioavailability of silibinin in rats was estimated to be 0.73%." — [Wu et al. 2007, J Pharm Biomed Anal, PMID 17692492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17692492/)
- **Rat, silipide vs silymarin [PR; authors from Inverni della Beffa/Indena].**
  - Dose: 200 mg/kg silybin equivalents.
  - Silipide Cmax: "9.0 +/- 3.0 micrograms/ml for unconjugated drug and 93.4 +/- 16.7 micrograms/ml for total."
  - "the biliary recovery after 24 h accounted for about 13% of the administered amount" for silipide vs "about 2%" for silymarin.
  - "The relative bioavailability of silipide... was 10-fold higher than that of silymarin."

  — [Morazzoni et al. 1993, PMID 8149949](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8149949/)
- **Human, IdB 1016 (silipide) vs silymarin [PR; one author from Inverni della Beffa/Indena].**
  - Single doses "equivalent to 360 mg silybin" in "9 healthy volunteers".
  - "the bioavailability of IdB 1016 was much greater than that of silymarin."
  - "the terminal half-life was relatively short (generally less than 4 h)."
  - "Most of the silybin present in the systemic circulation was in conjugated form. Less than 3% of the administered dose was accounted for by urinary recovery of free plus conjugated silybin."

  — [Barzaghi et al. 1990, PMID 2088770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2088770/)
- **Human, low dose of silipide [PR]:** After 80 mg silybin as silipide, "Free silybin concentrations reached a peak of 141 +/- 31 ng/ml (mean +/- SEM) at 2.4 hours... half-life of about 2 hours. Peak concentrations of conjugated silybin were greater (255 +/- 35 ng/ml)." — [Gatti & Perucca 1994, PMID 7874377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7874377/)
- **Kidd & Head 2005 review [Review; published by Thorne Research, which sells Siliphos products]:**
  - "phytosomal silybin was absorbed 4.6 times better than the non-phytosome silybin from silymarin." This refers to Barzaghi's AUC.
  - Bile study in cholecystectomy patients: "the total amount recovered in the bile after 48 hours accounted for 11 percent of the total dose. In the case of silymarin, approximately three percent."
  - The review says "eight" volunteers; the Barzaghi abstract says 9.

  — [Kidd & Head 2005, PubMed 16164374](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16164374/); [full-text copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-review-of-the-bioavailability-and-clinical-efficacy-of-16qnu1qct3.pdf)
- **Human SPC soft-gel vs silymarin tablet, fasted, crossover, n = 23 [PR].**
  - Doses: 45 mg SPC soft-gel (NeoCholal-S) vs 70 mg silymarin (Legalon).
  - Cmax: 207.1 ± 62.4 vs 12.6 ± 31.4 ng/mL.
  - AUC0–∞: 308.8 vs 29.5 ng·h/mL.
  - "SIB bioavailability is 9.6 times higher with SPC."
  - The two arms had unequal silybin doses.

  — [Méndez-Sánchez et al. 2019, BMC Pharmacol Toxicol, PMC6330464](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6330464/)
- **Systematic review [Review]:** "rapid conjugation in intestinal cells or the liver" and efflux limit bioavailability. "Only about 1%-5% of the administered dose is eliminated by the kidneys." — [Tvrdý et al. 2021, Med Res Rev, PMID 33587317](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33587317/)
- **Secondary claim, not verified at source:** "The absorption rate of SM varies between 20 and 50%." This sentence is in the introduction of Méndez-Sánchez 2019 and refers to the fraction absorbed, not absolute F. — [PMC6330464](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6330464/)

### Inferences
- **For dogs the honest answer is "absolute F unknown."** The nearest analogue is the same Marin SPC in fasted cats, at about 7%. Two caveats apply:
  - Cats glucuronidate poorly, and the cat assay measured parent silibinin, so 7% may overstate what a dog achieves.
  - The cat paper is internally ambiguous. If "10 mg/kg of SPC" means SPC mass (about 3.1 mg/kg silibinin), the reported AUCs would imply about 24%. The stated 7% fits an oral dose of about 40–50 mg silibinin per cat, roughly one 40 mg capsule. [Extrap]
- **A plausible range for parent silybin reaching the circulation from SPC is about 0.6–7%.** The bounds come from horse SPC (0.6% in feed, 2.9% by tube) and cat SPC (7%). If rat plain-silibinin F (0.73%) is multiplied by the dog SPC advantage (3–4.4 times), dog SPC would be about 2–3%. [Extrap, low confidence]
- **"Reaching blood" has two meanings.**
  - Parent (unconjugated) silybin reaching the systemic circulation: about 1–7%, as above.
  - Silybin absorbed from the gut in any form, conjugates included: larger. With SPC, at least 11–13% of the dose is later recovered in bile (human 11% over 48 h; rat 13% over 24 h), plus urine (under 3% in humans). This suggests SPC absorbs at least about 11–16%, against about 2–3% for silymarin. [Extrap from human and rat data]
- **Cross-study numbers in dogs are not interchangeable.** The Legalon study (Li 2010) reports Cmax 1.06 µg/mL at 50 mg/kg silymarin, fasted, probably free silybin. Filburn's silymarin arm reports 0.47 µg/mL total silybin at 16 mg/kg silybin, fed. Assays, doses and feeding states all differ.

### Gaps
- **No canine IV silybin pharmacokinetic study was found**, so absolute F in dogs cannot be computed. Where I looked: PubMed, Europe PMC (searched "silybin/silibinin/silymarin AND beagle/dog AND pharmacokinetic/bioavailability"), and reviews (Tedesco 2023, Javed 2011, Hackett 2013 abstract).
- **No IV-referenced absolute F for silybin in humans was found.**
- The Hackett 2013 JVIM review full text could not be retrieved (Cornell mirror 403; publisher). Its statement on cat bioavailability and any dog data it gives are unverified.

## Q3. Food effect: does an empty stomach help or hurt silybin absorption from SPC?

### Takeaway
**No fed-versus-fasted silybin study exists in dogs, and I found none for SPC in humans.**
- The only canine SPC data (Filburn) were very likely obtained **after food**.
- The cat data (F ≈ 7%) were obtained **fasted**.
- In horses, SPC given **in feed** was about 5 times less bioavailable than by nasogastric tube.
- The Denamarin instruction to give the product on an empty stomach applies to all three lines. It appears driven by SAMe (the manufacturer's SAMe curve is labelled "fasted"), not by silybin data.

For silybin, the evidence is insufficient to say whether fasting helps or hurts.

### Cited Findings
- **Dogs, fed dosing in the only SPC pharmacokinetic study [PR-ind]:** "Dosing following food intake was necessary at these higher levels for full acceptance of the supplement." Also: "It is recommended to administer the supplement on a full stomach." This recommendation was made because of zinc, not silybin. — [Filburn et al. 2006](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Cats, fasted dosing [PR]:** "Prior to both the oral and intravenous portions of the study cats were held off food for 12 hours." Result: "bioavailability of SPC administered on an empty stomach in a capsule form to cats is between 6-7%." — [Webb et al. 2012](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol10Iss2/Vol10%20Iss2Webb.pdf)
- **Horses, feed vs tube [PR]:** "0.6% PO in feed and 2.9% via nasogastric tube." — [Hackett et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24066917/)
- **Humans, fasted protocol in a modern SPC study [PR]:** "After a 10-h fasting period, they received a 45 mg dose of SPC... All volunteers fasted for 4 h after drug administration." — [Méndez-Sánchez 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6330464/)
- **Humans, clinical silipide given between meals [Review]:**
  - "Daily intakes ranged from 240-360 mg silybin in phytosome form, taken for up to 150 days between meals."
  - "an intake of 160 mg silybin daily (one 80-mg capsule twice daily, taken between meals) provided a good maintenance intake."

  — [Kidd & Head 2005](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-review-of-the-bioavailability-and-clinical-efficacy-of-16qnu1qct3.pdf)
- **Humans, lipid vehicle [Review]:** In 12 subjects given 80 mg silybin as phytosome, "The maximum plasma concentration attained from the softgel was three times greater than from the hardgel." — [Kidd & Head 2005](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-review-of-the-bioavailability-and-clinical-efficacy-of-16qnu1qct3.pdf)
- **Search for human food-effect studies:** a Europe PMC title/abstract search for silybin, silibinin, silymarin, silipide or milk thistle together with "food effect", "fed state", "high-fat meal", "with food" and similar terms returned no food-effect pharmacokinetic study. — [Europe PMC REST search](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search) (query documented in Gaps)
- **Original Denamarin label, distributor detail aid [Mfr]:** "Tablets should be given on an empty stomach at least one hour before a meal for optimum absorption (an overnight fast is preferred). If necessary, the tablets can be disguised in a small amount of food for easier administration, however this may impact the product's bioavailability." The supporting chart is "Bioavailability of SAMe – The mean (fasted) plasma SAMe concentration time-course after administering Denamarin." — [Detail aid](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/4028-denamarin_vet_detail_aid-lr-singles.pdf)
- **Denamarin Advanced, tablets and chewables [Mfr]:** "should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption." Also: "If pilling the dog is not possible, the tablet may be administered with a small amount of food." — [Banfield, Advanced Tablets](https://shop.banfield.com/denamarin-advanced-tablets-cats-dogs.html); [Banfield, Advanced Chewables](https://shop.banfield.com/27063130BO.html)
- **Regular Denamarin Chewable [Mfr]:** "should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption." — [1800PetMeds listing](https://www.1800petmeds.com/dog/otc-vitamins-and-supplements/product/nutramax-denamarin-liver-health-supplement-same-silybin/prod13991.html)
- **Skorupski RCT [PR-ind]:** owners were "instructed to administer Denamarin once daily on an empty stomach at the dosage recommended on the package insert." — [Skorupski et al. 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The empty-stomach rule is a SAMe rule.** For SPC-silybin itself, the canine data used to market Denamarin were generated after food. So nothing in the dog data shows that fasting improves silybin absorption. [Extrap]
- **Indirect hints point both ways:**
  - Toward food helping: a lipid or oily vehicle raised human Cmax 3-fold (soft-gel vs hard-gel). Phytosome dispersion plausibly benefits from bile or lipid.
  - Toward food hurting: in horses, a feed matrix lowered F from 2.9% to 0.6%. Horse digestion differs greatly from dog digestion, though.

  Neither is canine evidence. Net: **unknown direction and magnitude for dogs.** [Extrap]
- **For the comparison table:** label silybin numbers derived from Filburn as "fed-state reference, product given fasted per label."

### Gaps
- No canine or human fed-versus-fasted study of silybin or SPC was found. Where I looked:
  - Europe PMC title/abstract query: (silybin OR silibinin OR silymarin OR silipide OR "milk thistle") AND ("food effect" OR "fed state" OR "high-fat meal" OR "fed conditions" OR "with food" OR "food intake" OR "fasting and fed" OR "fed and fasted") AND (pharmacokinetic* OR bioavailability OR absorption).
  - Kidd & Head 2005.
  - Javed 2011.
  - Tedesco 2023.
- The feeding state of the silymarin comparator arm in Filburn 2007 is unverified.

## Q4. Do the three Denamarin lines use the same SPC? Silybin claims; data comparing the products

### Takeaway
All three lines list a "silybin-phosphatidylcholine complex (SPC)" that contains about 29% silybin A+B by weight. This is consistent with one shared SPC ingredient, the same type as Marin's SPC (about 31%), but Nutramax does not name the source. The silybin claims are identical across lines ("more bioavailable in dogs than standardized milk thistle extract") and trace back to the Marin dog study. The Advanced line's "better absorption" claim is about **SAMe**, not silybin. **I found no data comparing silybin absorption between the coated, Chewable and Advanced products.**

- **The SPC appears to be the same, or an equivalent, ingredient across all lines**, because the silybin fraction is constant at 28.5–29.8%. **24 mg silybin A+B from a coated tablet, a regular chewable, or a whole Advanced medium tablet should be treated as the same nominal silybin dose.** Whether a dog absorbs it equally from each is untested. Possible differences:
  - the coated tablet (protective or enteric coating) may change release timing;
  - the chewable matrix may differ;
  - co-ingredients differ (SAMe salt type and amount).

- **Which "half a tablet of Denamarin Advanced" the dog took matters:**

  Because the label prescribes ½ chewable for this weight, ½ chewable (17.5 mg) is the more likely regimen. **Confirm with the owner.** [Extrap]

## Q5. Can "milk thistle" and "silybin" share one column? Silymarin composition; SPC mg vs silybin mg

### Takeaway
**No.** The three terms name different things:
- **"Milk thistle"** can mean the fruit or seed (silymarin is a few percent of it), an extract (often 55–85% silymarin), or silymarin itself.
- **Silymarin** is a mixture in which silybin A+B makes up only about 50–60%.
- **SPC** is about 29–30% silybin by weight.

In addition, the same milligrams of silybin give about 3 times the exposure when delivered as SPC rather than as silymarin extract (dog data). A comparison should therefore normalize every product to **mg silybin A+B** and state the **form** (SPC vs extract).

### Cited Findings
- **Composition of silymarin [Review]:** "silibinin (50 – 60%), isosilibinin (or isosilybin) (~ 5%), silycristin (~ 20%), silydianin (~ 10%), and other components such as taxifolin (~ 5%)." Also: "The minimum content of SIL in mature milk thistle fruit is 1.5% (expressed as silibinin) and often ranges between 1 – 8% of dry matter." — [Tedesco & Guerrini 2023, PMC11961297](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11961297/)
- **Extract standardization, Nutramax patent [Patent]:** "silymarin may be present in the extract in an amount from about 55% to about 85% by weight of the extract." The family patent gives silybin A+B as "most preferably about 28% by weight of the extract." — [US7563779B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7563779B2/en); [US6863906B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6863906B2/en)
- **Phospholipid complexes:**
  - IdB 1016 (Siliphos) "is provided as one part silybin and two parts phosphatidylcholine." — [Javed et al. 2011, Altern Med Rev](https://e-lactancia.org/media/papers/CardoMarianoSilimarina-AltMedRev2011.pdf)
  - Siliphos specification: ≥29.7% to ≤36.3% silybin. — [Indena](https://www.indena.com/indena_files/2025/05/siliphos_ss_int.pdf)
  - Denamarin labels: 82 mg SPC provides 24 mg silybin A+B. — [PetSmart](https://www.petsmart.com/dog/health-and-wellness/vitamins-and-supplements/nutramax-laboratories-veterinary-sciences-denamarin-dog-liver-health-supplement-medium-dogs-30ct-84087.html)
- **Form changes exposure at equal silybin (dogs) [PR-ind]:** SPC vs "standardized silymarin extract containing equivalent levels of silybin" gave AUC 11,200 vs 3,720 (and 2,520) ng·h/mL. — [Filburn 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348898/)
- **Other components of silymarin are also absorbed [PR]:** "Plasma and bile obtained after administration of silymarin contained also silydianin, silycristin and, to a greater extent, isosilybin. The concentrations of the latter compound in plasma and in bile were higher than those of silybin itself." — [Morazzoni 1993 (rats)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8149949/)
- **Labels sometimes express silymarin "as silibinin" [PR, interpretation note]:** Tedesco states the fruit content of silymarin "(expressed as silibinin)". This shows that pharmacopoeial assays report silymarin as silibinin equivalents, not as true silybin A+B. — [Tedesco & Guerrini 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11961297/)

### Inferences
- **Conversions for the table [Extrap]:**
  - 82 mg SPC is **24 mg silybin A+B**, not 82 mg.
  - "100 mg milk thistle extract (80% silymarin)" is about 40–48 mg silybin A+B if silybin is 50–60% of silymarin, or about 28 mg using Nutramax's patent figure of 28% of the extract.
  - "100 mg milk thistle seed" is about 1.5–8 mg silymarin, which is under 5 mg silybin.
- **Bioavailability adjustment:** Even after normalizing to silybin, SPC-silybin gives about 3 times (2.8–4.4) the systemic exposure of extract-silybin in dogs. "Equivalent silybin" is therefore not "equivalent absorbed silybin." [Extrap from Filburn]
- The task brief's figure of "silybin A+B ≈ 50–70% of silymarin" is broader than the 50–60% given by Tedesco. 50–60% is the better-sourced range.

### Gaps
- The silybin content of non-Nutramax "milk thistle" products cannot be generalized. It must be read from each label, and many labels give only "milk thistle extract" mg or "silymarin" mg.

## Q6. Converting to absorbed amounts and expected plasma levels for a 9.1 kg dog

### Takeaway
**Every number in this section is an extrapolation.** Linearly scaling Filburn's fed-state data from 16 mg/kg down to 2.64 mg/kg (24 mg in a 9.1 kg dog) predicts:
- total-silybin **Cmax ≈ 0.2 µg/mL (about 216 ng/mL; ±1 SD 71–361)**;
- **AUC0–24 ≈ 1,850 ng·h/mL (770–2,920)**.

If dogs resemble horses and cats (F 0.6–7%), the **parent silybin that reaches the systemic circulation is about 0.15–1.7 mg** of the 24 mg. Silybin absorbed in any form, mostly as conjugates, is probably larger: about 2.6–3.8 mg (11–16%) by analogy with human and rat recovery data. Uncertainty is large, roughly ±1 order of magnitude for parent silybin.

### Cited Findings
- **Reference dog data [PR-ind; Mfr]:** SPC at about 16 mg/kg silybin, fed (Filburn): Cmax 1,310 ± 880 ng/mL, AUC0–24 11,200 ± 6,520 ng·h/mL. Silymarin extract at an equal dose: Cmax 472 or 449 ng/mL, AUC 3,720 or 2,520 ng·h/mL. — [PubMed 17348898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348898/); dose from [detail aid](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/4028-denamarin_vet_detail_aid-lr-singles.pdf) and [Filburn 2006](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Nonlinearity warning [PR]:** In horses, "silibinin had nonlinear pharmacokinetics." — [Hackett 2013 AJVR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24066917/)
- **F values available for bounding [PR]:**

  | Species | Form | F | Source |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Cat | SPC, fasted | ≈7% | [Webb 2012](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol10Iss2/Vol10%20Iss2Webb.pdf) |
  | Horse | SPC | 0.6% in feed; 2.9% by tube | [Hackett 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24066917/) |
  | Rat | plain silibinin | 0.73% | [Wu 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17692492/) |

- **Lower bounds on absorption in any form:**
  - Human SPC: 11% of the dose in bile over 48 h. — [Kidd & Head 2005](https://typeset.io/pdf/a-review-of-the-bioavailability-and-clinical-efficacy-of-16qnu1qct3.pdf)
  - Human: under 3% in urine. — [Barzaghi 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2088770/)
  - Rat silipide: about 13% in bile over 24 h. — [Morazzoni 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8149949/)

### Inferences

Method and assumptions:
- Linear dose scaling from a 16 mg/kg reference (alternative references: 14.25 mg/kg or 17.55 mg/kg, from 178 mg across the 10.14–12.49 kg body-weight range).
- Fed-state reference; healthy beagles; "total" silybin.
- Ranges are ±1 SD scaled.

Effect of the choice of reference dose (24 mg row): using 14.25 mg/kg gives Cmax ≈ 242 and AUC ≈ 2,070; using 17.55 mg/kg gives Cmax ≈ 197 and AUC ≈ 1,680.

- **Sources of uncertainty, roughly in order of importance:**
  1. Absolute F in dogs is unknown. The F range spans more than 10-fold, and the values come from cats, horses and rats.
  2. Dose proportionality is untested in dogs. The reference dose was about 6–7 times the label dose, and horses showed nonlinearity. Saturable conjugation would make low-dose exposure lower than linear scaling predicts; saturable efflux would make it higher.
  3. Feeding state differs: the reference was fed, the labels say fasted, and the direction of any effect is unknown (Q3).
  4. The product differs: Marin (SPC plus vitamin E plus zinc, after food) vs Denamarin (SPC plus SAMe; coated tablet, chewable, or Advanced matrix).
  5. Dogs vary widely: CV 58–67% in Filburn.
  6. Disease state: hepatic dysfunction or cholestasis could raise plasma silybin. In humans with extrahepatic biliary obstruction, total-silybin AUC was more than 40 times free-silybin AUC (Javed 2011 citing Schandalik).
  7. The "total silybin" assay used background subtraction.
- **The relative comparison is the most defensible statement:** silybin dose ratios across the three regimens of 24 : 17.5 : 12 = 1 : 0.73 : 0.5. Because all are the same SPC, expected exposure should scale in roughly the same proportions, if absorption from each matrix is equal.

### Gaps
- There is no canine dose-ranging silybin study, no canine IV study, and no pharmacokinetic study in dogs with liver disease, so none of the extrapolations above can be validated.
- Half-life in dogs is not reported in the abstract. The 2006 paper says levels "return to background levels at 24 hrs." Cat t½ is about 2.5–3.2 h; human t½ is under 4 h, or about 2 h for free silybin.

## Q7. Evidence on hepatoprotective silybin doses in dogs (brief)

### Takeaway
Label-implied maintenance doses of SPC-silybin are about **2–2.5 mg/kg/day**: Marin gives 24 mg to a 10–12.5 kg dog, and Denamarin's medium size covers about 1.5–4.4 mg/kg across its weight band. A 9.1 kg dog on 24 mg/day receives 2.64 mg/kg. The only canine clinical RCT used Denamarin at label dose, once daily, fasted (Skorupski 2011, Nutramax-funded). Other canine studies used silybin at about 1–3 mg/kg/day (supplements) up to 50 mg/kg (Amanita rescue). I could not verify the often-quoted "2–5 mg/kg/day" range against a primary source.

### Cited Findings
- **Marin label dose [PR-ind]:** "The recommended administration for the size dogs used in this study [10.14–12.49 kg] is ... 24 mg silybin." That works out to about 1.9–2.4 mg/kg/day [Extrap]. — [Filburn 2006](https://jarvm.com/articles/Vol4Iss4/Griffin.pdf)
- **Skorupski 2011 RCT [PR-ind; Nutramax-funded; Griffin employed by Nutramax]:**
  - Dosing: "once daily on an empty stomach at the dosage recommended on the package insert."
  - "Increased liver enzyme activity occurred in 84% of dogs receiving CCNU alone and in 68% of dogs on concurrent Denamarin."
  - Dogs receiving CCNU alone were "significantly more likely to have treatment delayed or discontinued because of increased ALT activity."

  — [PubMed 21689156](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21689156/); [full-text PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- **Doses in other canine studies, as summarized by Tedesco & Guerrini 2023 [Review]:**
  - Kocatürk 2016: "SAMe 20 mg + silybin 1 mg/kg-BW" (Denamarin) attenuated LPS-induced rises in liver enzymes.
  - Sgorlon 2016: milk thistle "1.5 mg/kg-BW" silybin for 60 days in 8 dogs with liver damage; ALT reduced.
  - Hepaxan: "28.3 mg/10 kg-BW of silybin" (about 2.8 mg/kg) for 30 days in 15 dogs; AST, ALP, GLDH and GGT decreased.
  - Overall: "For dogs, the analyzed literature reported a range of SIL at a dose of 10 mg/kg-BW and of 10 – 140 mg/kg-BW."

  — [PMC11961297](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11961297/)
- **Amanita poisoning [PR]:** silibinin 50 mg/kg given 5 and 24 h after intoxication; no treated dogs died. — [Vogel 1984](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6719456/)

### Inferences
- **The two Denamarin options at 24 mg/day sit at the label-intended level** (2.64 mg/kg/day). The earlier regimen of ½ Advanced was about 1.9 mg/kg (chewable) or 1.3 mg/kg (hard tablet). That is below the label dose only if it was the hard tablet. [Extrap]
- **No canine dose–response data for silybin exist.** "Hepatoprotective dose" in dogs rests on manufacturer labels plus one industry-funded RCT of a combined SAMe and silybin product, so silybin's own contribution cannot be separated (the Skorupski authors make the same point).

### Gaps
- I did not access Plumb's Veterinary Drug Handbook (milk thistle monograph) or the BSAVA formulary "Silybin" entry (BSAVA returned 403). Their dose ranges, including "2–5 mg/kg/day", are therefore unverified.
- The Kocatürk 2016, Sgorlon 2016 and Hepaxan studies were not read in full; the figures above come from the Tedesco review.
