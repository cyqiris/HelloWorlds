# Denamarin coated vs Chewable vs Advanced: clinical evidence and practical use for a 9.1 kg dog with chronic hepatopathy after cholecystectomy (on ursodiol)

Evidence tags used in every bullet: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed full paper; **[PR-ABSTRACT]** = conference abstract printed in a JVIM supplement (not full peer review; author affiliation noted); **[CONSENSUS]** = ACVIM consensus statement (peer-reviewed, expert opinion); **[EXPERT]** = specialist reference text or proceedings; **[MFR]** = Nutramax (manufacturer) statements; **[DIST]** = distributor, retailer or pharmacy copy (often reproduces the manufacturer's text); **[INDEP-TEST]** = independent product testing; **[COMMENTARY]** = blog or editorial; **[ANECDOTE]** = owner reports. Research done 2026-11-09. This is background for a conversation with the dog's veterinarian, not veterinary advice.

## 1. Which clinical studies used Denamarin, which formulation, and is there any study of the Chewable or Advanced?

### Takeaway
Only one peer-reviewed clinical trial has tested Denamarin as the intervention: Skorupski et al. 2011 (JVIM). It was a manufacturer-funded, open-label randomized trial of lomustine (CCNU) hepatotoxicity prevention in dogs with cancer. The paper calls the product only "Denamarin" and does not say whether it was the coated tablet or a chewable. It cannot have been Advanced, which launched in about October 2017. I found **no peer-reviewed clinical study of Denamarin Chewable or Denamarin Advanced**. What exists for those products is Nutramax-authored ACVIM Forum abstracts that report single-dose blood-level (pharmacokinetic) data in healthy beagles. I also found no study of Denamarin, SAMe or silybin in dogs after cholecystectomy, and no randomized trial in naturally occurring canine chronic hepatitis.

### Cited Findings
**Skorupski 2011, the only Denamarin efficacy RCT [PR]**
- Citation: Skorupski KA, Hammond GM, Irish AM, Kent MS, Guerrero TA, Rodriguez CO, Griffin DW. "Prospective Randomized Clinical Trial Assessing the Efficacy of Denamarin for Prevention of CCNU-Induced Hepatopathy in Tumor-Bearing Dogs." *J Vet Intern Med* 2011;25:838–845. DOI 10.1111/j.1939-1676.2011.0743.x; PMID 21689156 — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21689156/); [full-text PDF hosted by Protexin](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- **Product and dosing, verbatim:** "Group A dogs were dispensed Denamarin[b] at the time of the first dose of CCNU. Their owners were instructed to administer Denamarin once daily on an empty stomach at the dosage recommended on the package insert throughout chemotherapy." Footnote b reads only "Nutramax Laboratories Inc, Edgewood, MD". No dosage form (coated or chewable) and no mg/kg dose is reported. — [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- The product is described as "a commercially available veterinary product containing a stable salt of SAMe and silybin in a phosphatidylcholine complex, both with established bioavailability in dogs." The supporting citations are footnote a (Griffin DW, Whalen MO, Filburn CR, "Bioavailability of a novel formulation of s-adenosylmethionine in Beagle dogs," ACVIM Forum 2009 abstract), ref 26 (Center 2005, Am J Vet Res) and ref 27 (Filburn 2007, J Vet Pharmacol Ther). — [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- **Design:** "Fifty dogs were enrolled in the clinical trial between April 2007 and October 2009. Twenty-five dogs were assigned to group A and 25 dogs to group B." The dogs had lymphoma, mast cell tumour or histiocytic sarcoma and normal ALT at entry. Mean weight was 28.4 kg (A) and 29.1 kg (B). Mean starting CCNU dose was 70.6 mg/m², and 68% of dogs received prednisone. Randomization used a computer-generated list. — [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- **Results, verbatim:**
  - "Seventeen dogs (68%) in group A and 21 dogs (84%) in group B experienced an increase in ALT activity above the reference range."
  - "One dog in group A developed a grade 4 increase in ALT activity compared with 7 dogs in group B."
  - "The mean highest ALT activity was 173 IU/L (range, 24–1,018) in group A dogs and 692 IU/L (range, 43–5,552) in group B dogs. Dogs in group B had significantly greater posttreatment increases in ALT (P = .003), AST (P = .01), ALKP (P = .009), and bilirubin (P = .02)."
  - "Seven dogs (28%) in group B required temporary or permanent discontinuation of CCNU treatment because of a grade 4 increases in ALT activity whereas only 1 dog (4%) in group A … This difference was statistically significant (P = .02)."
  - Source for all four quotes: [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- **Limitations stated by the authors:**
  - "Additional limitations to this study are the lack of placebo in the control group and lack of blinding of clinician as to which group each dog was assigned." The laboratory staff were blinded.
  - "Because the combination was studied instead of individual products, it is not possible to determine which nutraceutical, if any, may be a more effective hepatoprotectant."
  - "Because nutraceutical content and bioavailability can vary substantially among products, the findings presented here cannot necessarily be applied to other formulations of SAMe and milk thistle extract."
  - "This study was designed to evaluate Denamarin's ability to prevent or lessen, not reverse CCNU-induced hepatopathy."
  - Source: [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)

Two other authors accepted honoraria or travel reimbursement for presenting the work. — [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The specialist's claim is accurate in a narrow sense.** The "regular" (original-line) Denamarin is the only version with a peer-reviewed clinical trial behind it. The Advanced salt has only manufacturer PK abstracts, which the ACVIM panel explicitly called unpublished (see Q5).
- **That evidence is thin and indirect for this dog.** It is a single manufacturer-funded, non-placebo trial. It tested prevention of drug-induced enzyme rises during CCNU chemotherapy in large dogs (about 28 kg). It did not test treatment of established chronic hepatopathy, vacuolar change or post-cholecystectomy enzyme elevation.
- **Neither original-line form can claim that trial specifically.** Skorupski does not name the dosage form, so the trial cannot be credited to the coated tablet over the original chewable. All that can be said is that it used a pre-Advanced Denamarin.
  - The ACVIM consensus lists the 2009 Griffin abstract (Skorupski's bioavailability citation) among the dog PK studies of the butanedisulfonate and "granular tosylate" salts. That leaves open which salt and form the trial product contained.
- **Enzymes may stay elevated in this dog regardless of supplement.** Persistent liver-enzyme elevation two months after cholecystectomy is common (9/12 in Pike 2004). The MSD text lists comorbidities, such as cholangiohepatitis, endocrinopathy and hyperlipidemia, that keep enzymes high. So enzyme trends should not be read as a test of any supplement.

### Gaps
- **Coated vs chewable not directly compared:** No peer-reviewed head-to-head clinical or pharmacokinetic comparison of Denamarin coated tablet vs Denamarin Chewable was found (searched PubMed via E-utilities, Europe PMC full text, and Google-type web search).
- **Advanced has no clinical-outcome data:** No peer-reviewed study of Denamarin Advanced, and no clinical-outcome study of either Advanced form, was found.
- **2026 abstract not readable:** The 2026 ACVIM Forum abstract program (JVIM 2026;40(5):aalag196; [PMC13540732](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13540732/)) is indexed by Europe PMC as mentioning "NMXSS75A", but the PDF was bot-blocked and could not be read. It may hold a newer Nutramax abstract.
- **Original chewable launch date** not found, so I could not confirm whether a chewable existed during Skorupski enrollment (2007–2009).
- **Hagen 2019 dosage form and dose** not available from the abstract (full text not accessed).
- **No study in the dog's actual conditions:** I found no study of SAMe, silybin or Denamarin in dogs after cholecystectomy for mucocele, or in naturally occurring canine chronic hepatitis or vacuolar hepatopathy.

## 2. Recommended SAMe and silybin doses, and what each product delivers to a 9.1 kg dog

### Takeaway
References converge on **SAMe about 20 mg/kg once daily on an empty stomach** for the older (butanedisulfonate or tosylate) salts. The ACVIM table gives **8–10 mg/kg for the phytate salt** used in Advanced, with a footnote that this lower dose is unsupported by published data. For silybin in phosphatidylcholine complex, the ACVIM table says the dose is "not well defined" and gives **0.7–6 mg/kg/day**.

For a 9.1 kg (20 lb) dog:
- **One medium coated tablet or one original chewable** gives 225 mg SAMe (24.7 mg/kg) and 24 mg silybin (2.6 mg/kg). That is about 124% of the 20 mg/kg target.
- **One medium Advanced hard tablet** gives 82 mg (9.0 mg/kg), which is inside the phytate range.
- **The label's ½ Advanced chewable** gives 60 mg (6.6 mg/kg), which is below the ACVIM 8–10 mg/kg table range and Nutramax's own 8 mg/kg target.

### Inferences
- **The coated-tablet switch does not change the silybin dose.** The original coated or chewable tablet gives the same silybin (24 mg, 2.6 mg/kg) as one Advanced hard medium tablet. The main difference between products for this dog is SAMe salt and SAMe amount, not silybin.
- **If the dog was on ½ Advanced chewable, the switch raises the SAMe dose.** That label dose gave 6.6 mg/kg of phytate SAMe, below the ACVIM table range. One original 225 mg tablet (24.7 mg/kg) is squarely in the consensus 20 mg/kg dose for an established salt.
- **If the dog was on one Advanced hard medium tablet, the switch is roughly neutral on paper.** That tablet gave 9.0 mg/kg. It is exactly the 82 mg dose Nutramax reported as PK-comparable to 225 mg of the tosylate salt in beagles of about this size. However, that abstract showed only "no significant differences" in 18 dogs and reported no equivalence margins, so it does not prove bioequivalence.
- **All products fall inside the only consensus silybin range.** Every option gives 1.3–3.8 mg/kg/day of silybin A+B, inside the ACVIM 0.7–6 mg/kg/day for phosphatidylcholine-complexed silybin, which is itself labelled "not well defined."

### Gaps
- **"Silybin 2–5 mg/kg/day":** not found in any source I could access. The ranges found were 0.7–6 mg/kg/day (PC complex, ACVIM), 4–8 mg/kg/day (native extract, ACVIM) and 20–50 mg/kg/day (silymarin, Johnson 2010).
- **Plumb's** (subscription) and **BSAVA Formulary** (bsavalibrary.com returned 403) were not accessible. Their SAMe and silybin monographs are not quoted.
- **Merck/MSD chronic-hepatitis treatment page** was not retrieved; only the mucocele page was.
- **Clinician's Brief:** the "Daisy's Case" article URL returned 404, so no Clinician's Brief dose statement is quoted.
- **Salt of the current coated tablet** (butanedisulfonate vs tosylate) is not stated on the Nutramax pages I read.
- **Previous product unknown:** which Advanced form the dog actually took (hard vs chewable, whole vs half) is unknown; both are computed above.

## 3. Splitting, chewing and crushing: is chewing or quartering a coated tablet acceptable, and is the Chewable an equivalent substitute?

### Takeaway
On splitting and chewing, the sources do not conflict:
- **Nutramax, its distributors and a DACVIM proceedings author all say the enteric-coated tablet should be given whole,** not broken, split, crushed or crumbled. Dose changes should be made in whole tablets. The stated reasons are protection from stomach acid, moisture and oxidation.
- **No study measures absorption after a coated Denamarin tablet is chewed or quartered.** The effect of the dog chewing it open is unknown, and the manufacturer advises against it.
- **The original Chewable is the product Nutramax designed for chewing and splitting.** It is scored, dosed in quarter-tablet steps, and contains the same labelled 225 mg SAMe and 24 mg silybin per tablet as the medium coated tablet. Nutramax says it gives "quicker uptake … and less variability" than the coated tablet. That claim rests on manufacturer data, not peer-reviewed work, and the Chewable still has to be given on an empty stomach.

### Cited Findings
**Coated tablets: give whole**
- Nutramax FAQ [MFR]: "Remember, Denamarin enteric-coated tablets should generally not be broken. ### Inferences
- **Chewing or quartering the coated tablet is off-label with unknown consequences.** It defeats the coating that Nutramax and its distributors say protects SAMe from acid, moisture and oxidation. Absorption could plausibly fall or become erratic, but no data quantify this. It is not a validated way to give the same dose.
- **For a dog that chews tablets, the original Chewable is the manufacturer-intended alternative.** It has the same labelled amounts (225 mg SAMe and 24 mg silybin; 1 tablet/day at 16–30 lb), it is meant to be chewed and split, and Nutramax claims faster, less variable uptake than the coated tablet.
- **"Equivalent" is supported only by label content and manufacturer PK claims.** No published comparison of clinical effect exists, and neither form can claim Skorupski 2011 specifically (see Q1).
- **The Chewable still needs an empty stomach** (see Q4). Wrapping it in a pill pocket is a deviation the original-line labels do not endorse. Only the Advanced labels explicitly allow "a small amount of food."
- **Splitting the Advanced hard tablet** is not addressed by Nutramax. The ACVIM describes the phytate salt as enteric-coated, so treat splitting it like splitting the coated tablet (not advised) unless Nutramax technical support (888-886-6442, listed on denamarin.com) says otherwise.

### Gaps
- **No absorption data after breakage:** no pharmacokinetic or stability data on chewed, crushed or quartered coated Denamarin tablets were found (searched PubMed, Europe PMC and the web).
- **No direct Nutramax technical-support statement found** on whether a chewed coated tablet "still works"; only the FAQ's "should generally not be broken."
- **Underlying chewable PK data unpublished:** the data behind the "quicker uptake… less variability" and Denosyl chewable-vs-EC claims are not public.
- **Compounded SAMe:** no veterinary source on compounding SAMe or Denamarin, or on its stability, was found.
- **Advanced hard tablets:** whether they are enteric-coated or scored is not confirmed on Nutramax pages.

## 4. Timing and interactions: empty stomach, ursodiol, Advanced with food, and a 3-meal schedule

### Takeaway
- **All four Denamarin products have the same primary instruction:** "on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal."
- **Only the Advanced labels add a fallback:** "may be administered with a small amount of food" if pilling is impossible. A Nutramax abstract (n=6) reported comparable SAMe levels for the new salt given with food versus tosylate given fasted, and said food "substantially" lowers absorption of the older tosylate salt.
- **A Cornell specialist's reference is stricter than the label.** It says SAMe should be given "after an overnight fast" and food withheld for 2 hours after dosing.
- **Ursodiol is the opposite:** references say give it **with food**.
- **No SAMe–ursodiol interaction data exist.** Nutramax says there are "no known interactions," and an older proceedings author suggests ursodiol and SAMe may be complementary.
- **Practical result:** give Denamarin first thing in the morning after the overnight fast, then wait at least 1 hour (ideally 2) before breakfast. Give ursodiol with a meal.

### Cited Findings
**Empty-stomach and food rules by product [MFR]**
- Coated: "Denamarin Coated Tablets should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption." — [denamarin.com coated](https://www.denamarin.com/coated-tablets)
- Chewable: "Denamarin Chewable Tablets should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption." — [denamarin.com chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/chewable-tablets)
- Advanced chewable: "should be given on an empty stomach, at least one hour before a meal for optimal absorption. If pilling the dog is not possible, the tablet may be administered with a small amount of food." — [denamarin.com Advanced chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/advanced-chewable-tablets)
- Advanced hard: same wording, plus "For cats, it is suggested that tablets should be followed immediately with a small amount of water to make sure the tablet passes all the way into the stomach." — [denamarin.com Advanced hard](https://www.denamarin.com/advanced-hard-tablets)
- Distributor copy of Nutramax text for Advanced: "Denamarin Advanced offers smaller tablets than original Denamarin that can be administered with a small amount of food." — [Covetrus, Advanced chewable S/M](https://www.northamerica.covetrus.com/Product/printdetails?sku=061007)

**Food effect data [PR-ABSTRACT, Nutramax]**
- "Previous studies have shown that when SAMe is administered with food there is a substantial decrease in SAMe bioavability (Table 1)." Table 1 is titled "Effects of Food Consumption on SAMe Tosylate Absorption."
- "The data indicate that this novel SAMe salt may be used in dogs with equivalent bioavailability when administered with food which may increase owner compliance."
- Source: [2016 abstract P02](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5260423/)

**Specialist timing guidance**
- MSD Vet Manual (Center) [EXPERT]: "SAMe (20–40 mg/kg, PO after an overnight fast, every 24 hours; food should also be withheld for 2 hours after dosing)" and "ursodeoxycholic acid (15–25 mg/kg, PO, divided every 12 hours and given with food)." — [MSD Vet Manual](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/digestive-system/hepatic-diseases-of-small-animals/canine-gallbladder-mucocele)
- ACVIM consensus [CONSENSUS]:
  - Ursodiol: "15 mg/kg once a day PO given with food to increase bioavailability."
  - SAMe: "20 mg/kg PO once a day on an empty stomach."
  - Ursodiol "is widely prescribed for liver disease despite little investigation of its efficacy," and "is indicated for dogs with CH manifesting evidence of cholestasis, inflammation involving bile ductules, and in those with suspected bile‐borne bacterial infection."
  - Source: [Webster et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6524396/)
- Johnson 2010 [EXPERT]: "Giving the drug on an empty stomach will also improve absorption"; "UCDA appears to be safe when used at a dose of 15 mg/kg PO q24 in both dogs and cats. The therapeutic effects of UCDA may be enhanced in the presence of SAMe." — [dvm360 proceedings 2010](https://www.dvm360.com/view/update-hepatoprotective-therapies-proceedings)

**Interactions**
- Nutramax FAQ [MFR]: "There are no known interactions of Denamarin with drugs or other supplements." — [denamarin.com FAQ](https://www.denamarin.com/frequently-asked-questions-about-denamarin-liver-health-supplements)
- Banfield, for Advanced [DIST]: "While there are no specific food or drug interactions reported for this product, potential interactions may still be possible." — [Banfield Advanced listing](https://shop.banfield.com/denamarin-advanced-tablets-cats-dogs.html)
- ACVIM consensus [CONSENSUS]: "Because silymarin can inhibit certain cytochrome‐p450 enzymes and p‐glycoprotein, caution is warranted if high‐dose silybin is used with polypharmacy protocols." — [Webster et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6524396/)
- Center 2005 [PR], the only dog co-administration PK data found (with prednisolone, not ursodiol): "plasma concentrations of SAMe or prednisolone were not affected by coadministration." — [PubMed 15757136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15757136/)

**Absorption time course (cat data only)**
- Cat PK of enteric-coated SAMe (Denosyl-SD4, 1,4-butanedisulfonate), dosed after a 12-h fast with food withheld 4 more hours: "Peak plasma concentrations develop at 2 to 4 hours." — [VIN, WSAVA 2002 proceedings](https://vin.com/doc/?id=3846407)

### Inferences
**Suggested schedule for a dog on three meals a day (to confirm with the vet)**
- **About 06:30:** Denamarin, original coated or Chewable, on the empty stomach left after the overnight fast.
- **No earlier than about 07:30:** breakfast (Nutramax's ≥1 h rule). Wait until about 08:30 if following Center's "withhold food 2 hours after dosing."
- **Ursodiol:** give with one of the meals (breakfast or dinner), as prescribed.
- **Lunch and dinner:** as usual.
- **Why this works:** the overnight fast is the easiest reliable empty-stomach window. Because ursodiol is given with food and Denamarin is not, they end up separated by at least an hour. That spacing exists for absorption reasons, not because of a known drug–drug interaction.

**Other notes**
- **Food allowance:** if the dog will only take a tablet in food, the Advanced line is the only Denamarin labelled for "a small amount of food." The supporting data are a manufacturer abstract in six beagles plus an equine abstract. For the original coated tablet or Chewable, food is expected to lower SAMe absorption by the manufacturer's own account ("substantial decrease" for the tosylate salt).
- **Silybin interaction risk:** at 24 mg/day (2.6 mg/kg) this is low-dose silybin. The ACVIM's polypharmacy caution is framed around "high-dose silybin."

### Gaps
- **"Empty stomach" after a meal undefined:** no Nutramax or specialist statement says how long after a meal the stomach counts as empty. Only "at least one hour before a meal" (Nutramax) and "after an overnight fast" (Center/MSD) were found.
- **No SAMe–ursodiol data:** no pharmacokinetic or interaction study of SAMe or silybin with ursodiol was found.
- **Food-effect magnitude missing:** the size of the food effect for the original tosylate salt (the 2016 abstract's Table 1 values) was not readable in the text version.
- **Chewable fed vs fasted:** no data on the original Chewable given with food.

## 5. Specialist opinion on Advanced vs original

### Takeaway
The most authoritative specialist statement is the 2019 ACVIM consensus panel (seven boarded internists and pathologists, including Center and Webster). It says the phytate salt's bioavailability details "have not yet been published." Its dose table flags that "Pharmacologic data not published to substantiate lower dose of the phytate salt." The panel also recommends SAMe products whose dog PK and content have been reported.

This supports the vet's colleague's view that the original line has more published support. That support is still mainly one trial plus older-salt PK. Nutramax's only evidence for Advanced is its own single-dose beagle PK abstracts. I found no independent specialist commentary arguing that Advanced is superior.

### Cited Findings
- **ACVIM consensus [CONSENSUS]:**
  - "Details of the phytate salt bioavailability have not yet been published, but it is thought to have increased bioavailability, permitting use at a lower dosage (5‐10 mg/kg)."
  - Table 19 footnote: "Pharmacologic data not published to substantiate lower dose of the phytate salt."
  - "Caution is advised in selecting a SAMe product because many have unknown bioavailability and vary in SAMe content."
  - "The panel recommends SAMe products from reputable manufacturers for which bioavailability and pharmacokinetics in dogs and product content have been reported."
  - "Although most panel members used a combination of SAMe silybin product, additional studies are needed to clarify the clinical benefit of silymarin products in dogs with CH."
  - Source: [Webster CRL, Center SA, Cullen JM, Penninck DG, Richter KP, Twedt DC, Watson PJ. JVIM 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6524396/)
- **Skorupski 2011 authors [PR]:** the findings "cannot necessarily be applied to other formulations of SAMe and milk thistle extract." — [Skorupski 2011 PDF](https://www.protexinvet.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/jvim-2011-skorupski-prospective-randomized-clinical-trial-assessing-the-efficacy-of-denamarin.pdf)
- **Nutramax's case for Advanced [MFR, PR-ABSTRACT]:**
  - "Initial pharmacokinetic research showed that the NMXSS75A™ S-Adenosylmethionine in Denamarin Advanced has greater bioavailability compared to the NMXSS75® S-Adenosylmethionine when used at the same dose."
  - "Greater bioavailability than original Denamarin."
  - The 2019 abstract: "the pharmacokinetics of 82 mg of the SAMe phytate compound was comparable to 225 mg of the SAMe tosylate compound" (n=18 healthy beagles, single dose, all authors Nutramax).
  - Sources: [Nutramax detailer](https://res.cloudinary.com/veso-apotek-no/image/upload/v1578478990/p_859842_documentation_1); [denamarin.com Advanced chewable](https://www.denamarin.com/advanced-chewable-tablets); [PMC6766498](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6766498/)

- **Johnson 2010 [EXPERT]:** "there is considerable variation in product purity, and lack of bioavailability may be an issue in some over-the-counter human SAMe products." — [dvm360 2010](https://www.dvm360.com/view/update-hepatoprotective-therapies-proceedings)
- **Second-hand report [ANECDOTE]:** an owner review relays that an internal medicine specialist said the supplement "may or may not help as research inconclusive." Seen only as a search-result excerpt; not verified. — [Chewy listing (search excerpt)](https://www.chewy.com/ca/nutramax-denamarin-s/dp/1000076999)

### Inferences
- **Why the colleague's claim holds:** the original line has a peer-reviewed RCT (Skorupski), and its earlier salts have peer-reviewed dog PK and pharmacodynamic data (Center 2005; Filburn 2007 for silybin). Advanced's phytate salt has only manufacturer abstracts, explicitly flagged by the consensus panel.
- **What the claim does not cover:** none of this shows better clinical outcomes in chronic hepatopathy for either line.
- **What Advanced offers:** a lower SAMe mass per dose and an explicit food allowance. Its claimed advantage is a pharmacokinetic surrogate (similar blood levels at a lower dose), not a clinical one.

### Gaps
- **VIN message boards and Rounds** (membership-only) were not accessible.
- **No university hospital handout** comparing Advanced and original was found.
- **Clinician's Brief "Daisy's Case"** (https://cliniciansbrief.com/article/daisys-case-supporting-liver-health-through-supplementation) returned 404. A search snippet suggested it is sponsored content, which I could not verify.
- **No independent PK data:** no independent (non-Nutramax) pharmacokinetic study of the phytate salt was found.

## 6. Safety and quality: adverse effects, NASC, independent testing, storage and blister packs

### Takeaway
- **Adverse effects:** reported as rare, mainly nausea. If vomiting occurs, labels suggest giving the dose with a little food, which conflicts with the empty-stomach goal.
- **SAMe product quality varies widely.** One UK study of veterinary SAMe products found more than a threefold spread in measured versus labelled SAMe. ConsumerLab's latest round of human and pet SAMe products all passed, but which pet product was tested is behind a paywall.
- **Storage:** SAMe degrades with heat and moisture. The coated tablet relies on its coating and foil packaging; Nutramax says the Chewable's salt technology removes the need for both.
- **NASC Quality Seal** status for Denamarin could not be confirmed.
